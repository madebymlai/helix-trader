# Writing a strategy

A strategy implements Nautilus' `Strategy` trait; backtest and live do not depend on a concrete strategy. One process runs one strategy. Use [`ema/`](ema/) and the maintained [`config/ema.toml`](../../config/ema.toml) as the non-model example.

## Authoring

1. Create `strategy/<name>/` with a `Part` for strategy-specific config and rules, a constructor, thin Nautilus wiring, and pure decision modules. Make `Part::reads` answer exactly the series the strategy subscribes to and uses for indicators.
2. Keep state in at least one Nautilus indicator (`ActorIndicator`) that the warmup feeds (`Warmup::feeding`), registered with none of Nautilus' indicators of the strategy: the strategy hands the warmup its bars from `on_bar` (`Warmup::on_bar`), and the warmup holds the live ones while a live warmup is pending (see [Warmup](#warmup)). So decide on a bar only once the warmup has ended (`Warmup::is_warming`) and the indicator, read directly, is initialized, as the EMA does. A model strategy's handle gates its decisions on its own features and target, none registered with Nautilus. Keep decisions free of I/O and look-ahead, use the strategy clock rather than the system clock, and seed randomness from config.
3. Build a `Warmup` from the data requirements, feed the indicators through it, and forward historical responses and time events as needed. Beside it, `subscriptions::Subscriptions` of the requirements' bar types, ticks and custom series owns every live subscription, a model strategy's adding its target's ATR's bars, which no strategy declares, made `live()` where the strategy is built for a live node, as its warmup is (`Warmup::live`), so that a custom series' subscription names its data client (see [Warmup](#warmup)): started from `on_start` with the time the warmup's start returns, it subscribes to the bars, to each tick series and to each custom series from where the warmup ends, the quotes of the `[trade] conversions` pairs among the ticks, whose history the warmup requests as it does every series of the requirements (see [Warmup](#warmup)). A strategy whose ticks or custom series on a root need roll handling (see [Configuration and series](#configuration-and-series)) holds a `rolls::Roller` of the ticks' instruments (`Roller::live`, `Roller::replayed`) and of the custom series' roots (`Roller::custom_roots`, whose contracts it loads none of), started after it from the same time, which alerts before each roll of a root and returns the transition from `on_time_event`: hand it to the subscriptions' `roll`, which moves the root's series to the post contract (see [rolls](../rolls/README.md)). A model strategy's handle holds all three, and its data hooks are `model_data_hooks!`, written once for every one. Use `strategy::orders::Trade` for trades, sent at a stake (unless the strategy does not size by risk, see [Configuration and series](#configuration-and-series)), refuse an inverse traded instrument (`orders::refuse_inverse`) and rearm open trades on start.
4. Export `pub fn visit<V: Visitor>(visitor: V) -> V::Output` from the folder's `mod.rs`, calling `visitor.plain::<S>()` for a strategy trading on no model (it implements `PlainStrategy`) or `visitor.model::<S>()` for a model strategy. Nothing else registers it: `build.rs` declares each folder of `strategy/` holding a `mod.rs` as a module and a command-line name, both the folder's name, a lowercase identifier. Non-model strategies refuse train and dataset commands. Test the pure decisions and config rules, then exercise wiring on a fixture backtest.

**Keeping a strategy private:** list its folder in `.git/info/exclude` (`/src/strategy/<name>/`), never in `.gitignore`, so that no committed file names it. A checkout without the folder builds without the strategy.

**Formatting:** the strategies reach the crate through the build script's generated `include!`, which `cargo fmt` does not follow. Format them with `rustfmt src/strategy/*/mod.rs` after `cargo fmt`.

**Model strategies:** for features, decisions, labels, training, stores, meta-labeling or version bumps, read the [model guide](../model/README.md). `model_ema/` and [`config/model_ema.toml`](../../config/model_ema.toml) are the example, and `model_cross/` and [`config/model_cross.toml`](../../config/model_cross.toml) that of an event filter. For run orchestration see [backtest](../backtest/README.md); for live operations see [live](../live/README.md).

Nautilus' `nautilus_strategy!` starts a strategy with `Strategy::on_start`, which reactivates GTD timers when `manage_gtd_expiry` is set, then `DataActor::on_start`, where the strategy's own start goes; it need not call the former.

## Configuration and series

`StandardConfig::load` parses and validates the strategy's TOML before a node starts. `StandardConfig` owns the common `[base]`, `[data]`, `[indicators]`, `[trade]` and, for model strategies, `[model]` tables; `Part` owns top-level strategy fields, instrument count, whether it trades on a model or sizes its trades by risk, default base, declared indicators and additional rules. Unknown keys are rejected, except that Nautilus' `bar_spec` deserializer ignores extra keys. Parsing reports its first error; subsequent validation collects violations. The loaded `[base]` claims the external orders of the `[trade]` instruments (`external_order_instrument_ids`), after any it declares, and no other instrument the strategy reads: a strategy returns its config's claims from `Strategy::external_order_instrument_ids`, as `EmaCross` and `ModelEma` do, Nautilus' default returning none, and the live node registers them as it adds the strategy (see [live](../live/README.md)).

`[data].series` is exactly what `Part::reads` says the strategy reads: missing, extra and duplicate series are errors, custom series included, so a strategy reading custom data in its `on_data` declares it in `Part::reads`. The EMA reads only the first bars series of its traded instrument. `[trade].instruments` lists each instrument traded by its `instrument_id` alone: no instrument has a size of its own, and a `trade_size` is refused as the config is read, at its value, pointing to `[trade].risk`. `[trade].risk` is the fraction of equity a trade risks at the config's stake, written as a percentage (`"0.5%"`) above 0% and below 100%; a bare number is refused, as it could be a fraction or a percentage. A strategy that does not size its trades by risk, one holding a target exposure such as a rebalancer keeping target weights, declares `Part::SIZES_BY_RISK` false: its `[trade]` takes no `risk`, which is refused as an unknown key, and `config.stake()` panics, as `config.model()` does for a strategy without a model. It sizes its orders itself through the plain Nautilus API (`portfolio().equity`, its positions from `cache()`, `order().market`, `submit_order`), not through `orders::Trade`. Every other strategy keeps the default and requires `risk`; a model strategy must, its model being priced at the reference stake. When no series prices a traded instrument (its bars, quotes or trades), its quotes are added to data requirements for execution, not to `[data].series`: Nautilus prices a market order from quotes, trades or external bars, so depth, book deltas or a custom series under its id alone would have its orders denied. Consumers use `data_requirements()` rather than reconstructing requirements from config. `[trade].conversions`, optional, lists the FX pairs the equity a trade is sized from is converted through into the traded instrument's P&L currency, by their instrument ids, all on one venue: `conversions = ["EURUSD.ICMARKETS"]` for an IC Markets account in EUR trading gold quoted in USD. Each pair is added to the data requirements in one form, the first its venue's history serves (`venues::serves`) of its own 1-minute BID and ASK bars, then its quotes (`venues::rate_series`), so fill-catalog fills it, a backtest replays it and a live warmup requests it, and a backtest reads the rate a live run reads. Bars come first: a rate needs one recent value, and bars are the lighter history. So an IB pair is read as its bars (`EUR/USD.IDEALPRO-1-MINUTE-BID-EXTERNAL` and `-ASK-EXTERNAL`), to the bar types; a cTrader pair as its quotes, to the ticks, as a traded instrument's are, cTrader's history being quotes alone. The strategy subscribes to a pair's bars as to any bar type of the requirements, so its `on_bar` gets them beside its own and decides on its own alone: the EMA's signal counts its traded instrument's bars only (see [orders and positions](#orders-and-positions)). Left out, nothing is converted and the requirements are as before.

Declare each indicator name and argument type in `Part::INDICATORS`, build it from `config.indicators`, and register it for its input. Indicator tables not declared by the strategy are rejected. The shared config validates the declared arguments and strategy rules. A model strategy's features are declared the same way; its code hands each feature's table to the feature set rather than building it, and the TOML marks no role (see the [model guide](../model/README.md)). An argument type is `Deserialize` and `Serialize`, since a feature's arguments enter the fingerprint.

Bars are time-aggregated internal bars; `from` makes them composite from external source bars. Internal source bars are rejected because live warmup cannot request them. Tick series may be quotes, trades, book depth or book deltas; custom data is no tick series, an instrument's included, but a custom series (below). A book comes in its venue's format, the one its live data streams (`venues::book_format`), in a backtest as live, and is subscribed in it (`strategy::subscriptions`): its deltas at its book type, asking no depth, so each client streams the whole book it gives, its depth at its levels; a replay subscribes so too, though it reads neither from a subscription that manages no book. Deltas of an L3 book (L3_MBO) are orders, each added, changed or removed by its id, as LSE gives a `GLBX` contract's or an `XNAS` stock's; deltas of an L2 book (L2_MBP) are no orders: OKX's are price levels, each a level's new size, and IB's the rows of its market depth, each by its position, and by the exchange posting it on SMART depth, an update able to move a row to another price; so a feature reading deltas reads orders on one venue, levels or rows on another, and is not portable across venues as is. Depth snapshots hold the levels a side their venue sends, ten on LSE and five on OKX; IB streams no depth, and a cTrader broker no book. Custom data is of one kind, a custom series, such as an LSE reference series or an instrument's custom data: an entry of `[data].series` written as the Nautilus data type it is (`CustomSeries`): `data = "Custom:<type name>"`, its `identifier` and, if its type's source asks for any, its `metadata`, as `DataType::try_new` takes them, `{ data = "Custom:LseRow", identifier = "US10Y", metadata = { source = "bond_yields", symbol = "US10Y" } }` for LSE's daily 10-year US yield, `{ data = "Custom:DatabentoImbalance", identifier = "GCZ26.GLBX" }` for a contract's imbalances, keyed by its id as Nautilus' Databento client keys an instrument's custom data. It brings no instrument, so nothing is defined, loaded or requested as an instrument for it. An identifier naming a root of the roll calendar (`GC.GLBX`, see [rolls](../rolls/README.md)) rolls: the series is read on the contract standing for the root, as the root's other series are, its data type that contract's, the contract's id its identifier, its type and metadata kept (`CustomSeries::at`); any other identifier stands for itself, a contract's (`GCZ26.GLBX`), a plain instrument's or one naming no instrument (`US10Y`). So data stored literally under a root's id cannot be read as one series; store it under another identifier. The identifier is the config's to choose and required: it keys the series in the catalog (`data/custom/<type_name>/<identifier>`) and on the message bus, where Nautilus writes a fill's rows and a backtest reads them, so without one every series of a type would share one path; a config naming rows already stored writes the identifier they are stored under. The metadata says what the type's source asks for, LSE's a `source` and a `symbol` alone, which its client checks (see [the adapter's reference rows](../../crates/lse/README.md#reference-rows)); empty metadata is none, Nautilus' data type of either having one topic. An entry is refused as the TOML is read, naming why and, in the error's line, the entry, if it has an unknown key (Nautilus' own `DataType` deserializer would take a `topic`), no identifier, an `instrument_id` in its place (pointing at `identifier`, an instrument's custom data being keyed by its id), an `instrument_id`, `bar_spec` or `from` beside its identifier, an identifier inside its metadata, which Nautilus reserves, or an identifier holding a backslash, which Nautilus' catalog writes as a folder separator and reads back as a character, so the series would be stored where no read finds it, or keying no catalog folder of its own, empty, blank or slashes alone once made the folder Nautilus names, `/` removed and `^` made `_`; a series of ticks or bars naming an `identifier` or `metadata` is refused so too. At validation it is refused under `data.series[i]` if an entry before it of its type is stored under the same folder (`US/10Y` beside `US10Y`), whatever its metadata, or shares one with it through a root: a root's series is stored under the folder of each contract standing for it, so a contract's series of its type listed beside it, before or after, is refused unless it is the root's on that contract, of the root's metadata, read once (`GCZ26.GLBX` of other metadata beside `GC.GLBX` is refused, `GCV26.GLBX`, which never stands for GC, is not; `rolls::root_of`), if Nautilus has no registration of its type, so a type-name typo surfaces at the config (Nautilus decodes custom data by its type's registered Arrow schema, `get_arrow_schema`, and helix registers every custom type it compiles once as the process starts, before any config loads, `venues::register_custom_data`), or if its type's row names a history source that does not serve it (`venues::serves_custom`), LSE's rows without their symbol say. A type with no row of the routing table, or a row with no history source, is the catalog's: data written into the catalog from outside helix, by a type compiled into helix and registered there. A custom series is routed by its type's row alone (`venues::CustomRow`), whatever its identifier: the row names its history and its live data source, each on its own, LSE's rows LSE for both. A model strategy reads those its features read (see [model features](../model/README.md#features-and-data)); its subscriptions subscribe to its data type as read at their start, naming no client in a backtest or a dataset run and its data client live (see [Warmup](#warmup)), a root's moving to the post contract at each roll (`strategy::subscriptions`), and its `on_data` gets the rows without the strategy subscribing itself. Data requirements carry each (`DataRequirements::custom`) as the config gives it, in a fill, a replay and live alike: fill-catalog fills it from its type's history source, LSE's rows from LSE, a root's contract by contract over each slice, and skips the catalog's, filling the rest; a backtest and a dataset run replay it over its slices of the window (`DataRequirements::custom_slices`), a root's contract by contract, a contract's series listed beside its root's read once, each row when its values are known (a date's at its end), the catalog's as any other, and a slice the catalog holds nothing of is warned of as Nautilus warns of any other series it reads nothing of (`No data found for config: DataType(...)`), the run going on; a live warmup would request it of its type's live data client, catalog first, or of the catalog alone if it is the catalog's, but a live run reading any its type's live data does not stream is refused before anything connects, naming it (`venues::streams_custom`): LSE streams none, and nothing streams the catalog's. The strategy subscribes to its own tick and book streams. Consult the [catalog guide](../catalog/README.md) for source availability and [backtest guide](../backtest/README.md) for replay.

A futures root's bars must be internal LAST bars built from its trades; orders must name a contract, not a root. Root tick subscriptions, custom series on a root and continuous bars require roll handling; see [rolls](../rolls/README.md) for calendars and transitions. A conversion pair's quotes on a root need none: a futures contract has no base currency, so Nautilus' rates leave it out, rolled or not (see [Orders and positions](#orders-and-positions)), and the EMA, whose only ticks are its pairs' quotes, holds no roller.

## Bars and sessions

Internal BID, ASK and MID bars are built from quotes, LAST bars from trades. A bar stamped at close contains ticks in `(open, close]`; quote-bar volume sums quoted sizes, not trades. LAST bars carry no spread. Composite bars aggregate source bars, so their high and low reflect those source intervals. Backtest and live disable empty time bars: a period without a tick or source bar produces no bar rather than a stale flat bar.

A configured session accepts a bar only when its whole span lies within the local day's open and close in the configured IANA time zone. Outside bars neither update indicators nor trigger orders, including during warmup. Omit the session for 24/7 trading; `00:00` to `23:59` excludes the bar closing at midnight. Sessions are rejected with composite bars: composite bars are built and stamped five seconds after close to allow source bars to arrive live, in a backtest and a dataset run as live. Quote-built bars have no such delay, unless the strategy also reads composite bars: the delay is the data engine's, for every time bar it builds.

## Orders and positions

A trade is built without a quantity: `Trade::new` takes its instrument, side, exits, the reference price its exits are set from, and one R, the distance in price from that reference to its stop, or an ATR for a trade ended by its time alone. Its fields are the order module's, so no other code changes its exits or its one R once it is built. A strategy owns how much it risks: the stake, the config's risk times a multiple, 1 at `config.stake()`, scaled by `stake.times(k)` for a positive `k`. `trade.send(strategy, stake, rates)` reads the equity of the account its orders are checked against as its broker reports it (see below), converts it into the instrument's P&L currency at `rates`, the config's (`config.rates()`), and sizes the trade with Nautilus' `calculate_fixed_risk_position_size`: the stake's fraction of that equity over what its stop is worth on one unit, rounded down to the instrument's size increment and capped at its maximum quantity, no commission counted. The stop it is sized at is one R from the reference on the instrument's tick grid, rounded away from the reference, so a size never risks more than its stake. A size rounding down to zero sends nothing and logs a warning; an equity the rates do not convert, holding a currency no listed pair reaches or with no rate cached yet, sends nothing and logs an error. Nautilus' risk engine judges what is sent: a quantity below the instrument's minimum, or a margin beyond the free balance, is denied, and the run goes on. Each entry is tagged with its R in money, its quantity's worth over one R (`R:500.00 USD`), and its stake's multiple (`MULTIPLE:2`), read back with `orders::entry_risk`, and with the bid and ask of the instrument's latest quote in the cache, if it holds one (`BID:0.70140`, `ASK:0.70160`), read back with `orders::entry_quote`, which a backtest's trades file carries (see [backtest](../backtest/README.md#trades-file)); a model decision's entry also carries its expected R (see [model strategies](../model/README.md#reference-stake-and-the-strategys-stake)). Inverse instruments are refused at start, since the sizer reads a P&L as a multiple of a distance in price. A model strategy sends its decision's trade the same way, at a stake and rates of its own (`decision.trade().send(self, stake, rates)`); its model is priced at the reference stake, the config's risk of the dataset balance converted at the decision's rate, through `trade.send_at(strategy, stake, equity, ids)`, which sizes at a fixed equity in place of the account's (see [model strategies](../model/README.md#reference-stake-and-the-strategys-stake)).

The account is the one Nautilus' risk engine checks the orders against (Nautilus' execution guide, `concepts/execution`, "Account selection"): the account the cache registers for the execution client routed for the instrument's venue, else for the default client (`Cache::client_id_for_venue`, then `Cache::account_id_for_client`), else the account issued under the instrument's venue. A live node routes each cTrader broker's venue and OKX to their own clients and makes IB the default client; a backtest routes each simulated venue to its own. IB's account is issued under `IB`, not the exchanges its instruments name (`IBIS`, `IDEALPRO`), and is found through IB's client. The equity is the account's balance plus the open P&L of its positions on the instrument's venue: positions the same account holds on its broker's other venues are left out, as are other processes', which the cache does not hold anyway, one process running one strategy; live on IB, the equity is the account's net liquidation, which counts every position of the account, on any venue and of any process. Where it is read from is its broker's answer in the venues table (`venues::equity_source`): the strategy's portfolio (`Portfolio::equity`, which adds the open P&L to a margin account's balance) for a cTrader broker, OKX and a backtest's simulated account, whose balances leave open positions out; the account's balance totals alone on IB, whose net liquidation holds that P&L already (see [IB](../venues/ib/README.md#trading-and-warmup)). An account with a base currency, a cTrader account's, reports its equity in that currency alone, which the trade converts. An account without one, OKX's, holds each currency apart, its balance in one and a position's open P&L in another: the trade converts each and sizes on their sum, not on the open P&L alone, a loss counting against the balance.

Conversion (`strategy::conversions`) reads Nautilus' own rates. Each non-zero amount of the equity is converted at Nautilus' cached rate on the pairs' venue, `Cache::get_xrate(venue, from, to, PriceType::Mid)`, the mid of the latest quote of each pair on the path Nautilus finds through the venue's pairs, or of the closes of its latest BID and ASK bars for a pair with no quote cached, an IB pair's, as Nautilus' portfolio converts, and the amounts are summed; an amount of zero needs no rate, so an empty balance or a flat P&L in a currency no pair reaches blocks no trade; Nautilus' fixed-risk sizer then takes the converted equity at an exchange rate of 1, since its own rate converts one amount and an equity may hold several currencies. The entry's R tag stays in the P&L currency. Without conversions, an amount in another currency than the P&L's is not converted. Before a pair's first quote or bars are cached there is no rate through it, so a decision then sends nothing; a live strategy subscribes to the pairs at its start (see [live](../live/README.md#fx-conversions)). The pairs of a broker whose account has a base currency are on the traded instrument's venue (see below); those of one whose account has none, IB's, may be on any one venue, only the sizer converting: gold on GLBX, which lists no FX pairs, traded through IB on an account in EUR converts through `conversions = ["EUR/USD.IDEALPRO"]` live, its backtest taking a balance in USD (see below). A model strategy's dataset balance is converted the same way, at each decision's rate, and refused by the check a backtest's balance passes (see below and [model strategies](../model/README.md#reference-stake-and-the-strategys-stake)).

A config whose conversions can never work is refused before a run, naming why. The config's load refuses a pair whose venue's history serves neither its 1-minute BID and ASK bars nor its quotes, an OKX pair, naming the pair and its venue, since no rate of it could be read; pairs on more than one venue, naming the venues, since a strategy converts at Nautilus' rates on one venue, its pairs'; for an instrument traded whose broker's account has a base currency (a cTrader broker's, `venues::has_base_currency`), a pair on another venue than the instrument's, naming both, since Nautilus converts that account's margins, commissions and P&L into its base currency only through rates on the traded instrument's venue, while for an account without one (IB's, OKX's) only the sizer converts, so the pairs may be on any one venue; and pairs listed by a strategy not sizing its trades by risk, which sizes no trade from equity, `conversions` being refused as an unknown key as its `risk` is. A backtest refuses before its run, reading the definitions in its catalog, a listed pair whose definition has no base currency, naming the pair, since Nautilus' rates leave such an instrument out, and a balance whose currency the pairs do not reach a traded instrument's P&L currency from, naming both currencies. On a broker whose account has no base currency (IB's, OKX's) it refuses too a balance in another currency than a traded instrument's P&L currency, naming the instrument, its venue and both currencies, since Nautilus books such a simulated account's losses and commissions only in a currency it holds, a limitation no upstream issue tracks (see [backtesting](../backtest/README.md#leverage-and-venues)); a model's dataset balance is not refused so, its dataset runs booking nothing, nor is a live run. A live run has no definitions before it connects: there a currency the pairs do not reach is a trade not sent, logged.

A trade's exits hold a `Bracket`, its take profit and stop loss, which produces a Nautilus bracket, or none, which produces a market entry. The time limit starts at send, not fill. Forward time events to `orders::on_time_event` and call `orders::arm_open_trades` on start so cached open trades regain their alerts after a restart. The alert cancels open orders and closes any remaining position; a partial exit does not end the remaining trade.

Leave `oms_type` unset so positions follow the venue. On a netting venue overlapping trades share a position and one trade's time exit closes the whole position, including the other trades' share. On a hedging venue each trade has its own position. The EMA is an example and permits overlap. Its bracket legs and time close are reduce-only except on IB, whose execution client denies them. On IB the two legs of a bracket are linked by IB itself, which reduces the other leg at a partial fill and cancels it at a full one even while the node is down; see [IB](../venues/ib/README.md#trading-and-warmup) for how and what overlap still risks there, and [backtest](../backtest/README.md) for execution limitations.

## Warmup

Live requests history at start and hydrates indicators before the live stream. A backtest requests no warmup: it replays only its requested interval, and indicators initialize on that replay. Start earlier if decisions are needed at a particular time. A dataset build replays the features' warmup before its first day and stores no earlier decisions; see the [model guide](../model/README.md).

`strategy::warmup::Warmup` owns the request protocol, and `strategy::subscriptions` the subscriptions, which start at the time the warmup's start returns, where its requests end. It requests the history of every series of the data requirements its venue's history serves (`venues::serves`, the rule fill-catalog checks its series by): a composite bar requests its external source bars, other bars the quotes or trades from which they are built, and each tick series is requested as it is, whatever reads it, a feature, a conversion pair's rate or a traded instrument's prices. Nautilus caches the quotes and bars of each response, so a pair has its rate at the start, a start while the market is closed included if the warmup reaches back to its close. A series its venue's history does not serve, an OKX instrument's quotes, is requested no history: the warmup logs a warning naming why, and the series, subscribed from the start as every series is, and the bars built from it start there; a series its venue's live data does not stream is refused before the node starts (see [live](../live/README.md#startup-and-routing)). A custom series is requested by its data type as read at the start, a root's the front contract's, asking no definition: of its type's row's live data client, catalog first and written back, if its type's history serves it, and of the catalog alone if it is the catalog's, data written into the catalog from outside helix, which a client not serving it would fail. Live, it is subscribed naming its data client on the node, its type's row's live data source's (`venues::custom_data_client`), LSE's for LSE's rows, so that Nautilus sends that client the subscription, routed by its client id alone, and the node has that client whether or not an instrument of the run routes to it (`venues::live_clients`); a type with no row, or a row with no live data source, names none, nothing streaming it, and a backtest or a dataset run names none, its engine having no such client. A venue's history is never asked of custom data, an instrument's included. First, the warmup asks the definition of each instrument of the series that the cache lacks, of the client the venue's row names (its broker's, else its live data source's): the catalog answers first, so `LSEDGE` definitions, which LSE does not publish, come from there, and those the broker clients already cached are left as they are. Every series is asked in windows of 23 hours through Nautilus' time-range requests, each answered and written back on its own, and a run of empty windows does not end one. The first window of a request of book deltas whose UTC day's start the catalog holds is read from there and opens with a snapshot of the book at the request's start, rebuilt from the deltas before it at the book type of its venue's format, as the live stream that follows it is subscribed; each later window, earlier windows' write-back included, is read from its own start and continues that book with no snapshot of its own, so a feature reading deltas sees one snapshot at the warmup's start, then the deltas as the venue's feed carries them. The bars a request builds, composite bars included, reach indicators window by window: Nautilus builds each window's bars into the cache before it hands the window on, and the warmup reads those it has not read yet at each response of the request, each once, merged in time order with the other series (below); a bar it builds at a request's completion from the live data Nautilus held for that request, closing after the warmup's end, is live, and joins the live ticks held, in time order. It keeps the subscription continuous across the request boundary and sizes the live cache for the requested bars. Root continuous bars have a separate path; live currently refuses them, as described in [rolls](../rolls/README.md).

A completed request does not itself enable decisions: indicator initialization does. A model strategy's decisions also wait for every warmup request to complete, as features reading ticks can be initialized on the older windows alone. Live ticks start at the subscription, while older windows are still being answered, and Nautilus holds them for its bar aggregators alone, and a bar aggregator's only until its own request completes, so the live bars it builds from then on come while other requests may still be answering. The warmup hands a model strategy's features the quotes and trades of its responses and the bars its requests build, and its indicators their bars, holds the live ticks and bars until the last window is answered, then hands them over in the order they came, dropping those the history holds (a bar at or before the history's last of its bar type); each feature reads those of its own series alone, so a pair's or a traded instrument's quotes no feature reads reach none. The bars' part of this hold is Temporary as the hold of ticks is (helix-trader#16, upstream nautilus_trader#5675) and as the merge is (helix-trader#46). Each series' history comes in the windows of its own request, in the order its venue answers them, so the warmup merges its history across series, Temporary until Nautilus hands a warmup's history in time order across data types (helix-trader#46): of the series the indicators it feeds read, each named as it is fed (`Warmup::feeding` and its siblings), a conversion pair's or a traded instrument's quotes no feature reads going on as they come, it hands the quotes, trades, depth, deltas and custom data of its responses, and the bars each request builds, on in `ts_init` order up to the earliest latest `ts_init` among the series whose requests are still pending, `ts_init` being the time a backtest replays by and Nautilus cuts a request's windows by (live dedup stays on `ts_event` within a series, a live tick's `ts_init` being its receipt), a request's bars standing at the latest data it brought, since Nautilus builds a bar only once data past its close comes; ties at one `ts_init` go as a backtest hands them, custom data first, then the ticks in the order of the strategy's catalog series, as a backtest reads them (by instrument, a source of the strategy's bars first, the target's ATR's bars not among them, then `[data]` order), then built bars, as a backtest builds a bar after the data of its instant; a series whose request completes or fails holds the others back no longer, and the group's end hands on everything held, so a feature reading several series, bars among them, reads their history as a replay hands it. The merge holds at most 2 GiB of history (`merge::HELD`, a budget of its own that no config sets, going with the merge), each tick and bar counted by its size and what it keeps on the heap, a batch of deltas its deltas, a depth snapshot its levels, a custom point its data; past it, the warmup ends incomplete naming the series held back and those it waits on, and the rest of its history is left out. It relies on each series answering in time order and checks it: a tick initialized before the latest its series brought, a response out of order or a window older than an earlier one, ends the warmup incomplete the same way, naming the series. The merge is separate from the hold of live ticks, which goes with helix-trader#16; the merge goes with its own issue. Historical callback errors are logged rather than stopping the run, and an unanswered request leaves its group unfinished. The catalog supplies available history and the live client supplies missing intervals where supported; see [live](../live/README.md) for operational details. Recursive indicators retain a decaying dependence on their initial state even after initialization; allow several periods of history when reproducibility matters.
