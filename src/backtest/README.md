# Backtesting

Backtest runs one strategy in a Nautilus `BacktestNode` over the configured catalog. See the [root quick start](../../README.md), command `--help`, and [architecture](../../docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) for process constraints. A commission (fixed per order, a rate on each fill's value, or an amount per unit of each fill's quantity) and starting balance are required; choose values appropriate to the venue and instrument. Seed strategy randomness.

## Replay and data

The inclusive `[start, end]` window is the entire replay: a backtest makes no warmup request. Start early enough for indicators to initialize. An off-boundary start can produce a partial first bar. Catalog series feed both the strategy's bars and the venue's fills; declared tick and custom series are replayed too, custom data of no instrument (`Backtest::custom`, `[data].custom` of the strategy's config, such as an LSE reference series) by its data type's identifier over the whole window, routed to the strategy by its data type, each row at the time its values are known: an LSE daily row at its date's end, so that a day's close reaches the strategy at the next day's 00:00 UTC. A custom type is decoded by its registered name: the run registers those the venues' sources serve, LSE's rows (`venues::register_custom_data`), and the code declaring another type's series registers it first. Stored book depth and funding are included where applicable. Roots expand into dated-contract slices; see [rolls](../rolls/README.md) for boundaries and continuous-bar restrictions.

The catalog is not checked for completeness before a run. Missing instruments can fail the node, but missing series may only produce a warning; partial coverage and gaps replay as quiet periods. Stored intervals are request bookkeeping, not proof of completeness. Inspect coverage before trusting results, including the earlier data needed for model dataset warmup. No data for an hour means no bar, not a synthetic flat bar.

Ordinary replay streams data in chunks. A run with custom data loads **all** series for the window at once because Nautilus' streaming node cannot retain the preloaded custom data across chunks. Large depth histories can therefore exhaust memory; reduce the window or data volume.

## Fills and costs

The default fill model adds no slippage. External LAST bars, including IB minute TRADES bars and OKX candles, drive fills without a quoted spread. A market order on a decision bar can fill at the last minute's close; the assumed open-high-low-close sequence determines which resting order fills first within a minute. This is not observed intrabar order and can be optimistic. Minute volume can limit the amount filled at the close, with the remainder one tick worse; thin or zero-volume periods are especially unreliable.

On quotes, buys fill at the ask and sells at the bid of the last quote. Resting orders are checked on each replayed quote, external bar and trade tick (Nautilus' `bar_execution` and `trade_execution`, both on by default), so a crossing between them can be missed. A gap can leave a stale quote in use; without an earlier quote an order is rejected. Quote sizes may be nominal rather than observed depth, and liquidity is not consumed across orders. Tick quotes give more detail than minute bars but do not remove these limitations.

Fixed commission is charged on an order's first fill; a rate and an amount per unit of quantity are charged on every fill, the latter rounded to the currency's precision a fill, a half cent to the even cent, as Nautilus rounds money (`--commission-per-quantity`, Pepperstone's per-lot charges: see [Pepperstone](../venues/pepperstone/README.md#margins-and-costs)). None combines a rate with a minimum, so choose the approximation carefully for IB orders. The commission is the run's one cost source (`backtest::Commission`): the venues' fee model is made from it, and a model strategy's dataset runs and target read the same one (see [targets](../model/README.md#targets-and-datasets)). Stored OKX funding is settled on open positions; absent funding is not checked or settled. The simulated settlement price and timing can differ from OKX, including orders placed at a funding boundary. See [OKX funding](../venues/okx/README.md#funding-and-margins) for availability.

## Leverage and venues

Each simulated venue a broker trades starts with the specified balance and uses its live position mode, which it takes from `venues::oms_type`: IC Markets and Pepperstone are hedging, other venues netting. A venue no broker trades, only read, such as `LSEDGE`, starts with nothing in the balance's currency and Nautilus' default position mode, so that it adds no money: a run reading its series ends with the balances and return statistics of the run without them, its empty account aside, which the report lists. Dataset runs hedge on every venue. The same leverage and fee model apply to every venue in a multi-venue run.

The margin model applies both the instrument margin and `--leverage` (default 1). IC and Pepperstone instruments already carry the venue's margin: use 1x unless deliberately modelling a further division. OKX instruments have margin 1, so set leverage to the account's per-instrument leverage. IB instruments have zero margins and lock no margin in this simulation. Orders exceeding free initial margin can be denied, but liquidation is disabled; the simulation does not reproduce venue liquidations.

## Report

PnL is realized and after commissions, including funding; open positions can leave unrealized PnL outside that figure. Costs are derived from fills and the last quote at or before each fill. After the run the report reads the replayed quotes from the catalog again, a batch at a time, so it holds no quote history in memory. Spread is the fill's distance from the quote mid, times quantity and multiplier; with no replayed quotes it is `n/a`, not zero proven spread. Cost per trade divides total commission and measured spread by fills. Gross PnL adds both back to realized PnL, so an open position's opening spread makes it too high. Nautilus nets a commission into a position's PnL only when it is charged in the position's settlement currency; one charged in another currency, such as `--commission "3 EUR"` on a USD instrument, is in no PnL, so the report lists it apart as `Commission outside PnL` and counts it in neither cost per trade nor gross PnL. Inverse instruments are refused by this calculation. Max drawdown is a negative fraction; some statistics can be unavailable with insufficient observations.

## Model retraining

For model strategies, `--models` selects the shared store and `--dataset-balance` supplies the balance for target-generating dataset runs; make it large enough that target orders are not denied. Before replay, the backtest builds missing dataset days and trains, in its own process, on the epoch-aligned retraining schedule, starting at the last scheduled time at or before the run; each dataset run is a Nautilus node of its own, disposed before the next starts. Existing models valid at a scheduled time are reused: a train there fits only the models lacking one, and none runs where every model has one (see [training](../model/README.md#training-and-acceptance)). A failed dataset build stops the backtest; training that keeps the current model reports why, continues, and will be retried on a later run.

A strategy never retrained, without `retrain` in `[model]`, trains at the backtest's start alone, where a model is missing; a later backtest of the span, or from a later start, trains nothing. It then refuses to run, naming the sides or the meta, when either has no model in use at the start, since it would trade nothing; a retrained backtest whose trains were all refused still runs. Its scores cover the span as one period; see [never-retrained scheduling](../model/README.md#scheduling).

Each decision uses the latest valid pair of sides published by one train, subject to their checks; an orphan side is not a pair. Scores after the report compare out-of-sample predictions and economics against baselines for each side and, where configured, the meta model. Periods without a model or enough observations have unavailable statistics. See [model scoring](../model/README.md#scores) for the metrics and their populations. Backtest models are valid from their scheduled training time, whereas live models become valid only after training finishes, introducing some optimism. See [model guidance](../model/README.md) for datasets, validity and authoring, and [live operations](../live/README.md) for live retraining.
