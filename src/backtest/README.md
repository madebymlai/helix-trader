# Backtesting

Backtest runs one strategy in a Nautilus `BacktestNode` over the configured catalog. See the [root quick start](../../README.md), command `--help`, and [architecture](../../docs/architecture.md) for process constraints. A commission (fixed per order, a rate on each fill's value, or an amount per unit of each fill's quantity) and starting balance are required; choose values appropriate to the venue and instrument. Seed strategy randomness.

## Replay and data

The inclusive `[start, end]` window is the entire replay: a backtest makes no warmup request. Start early enough for indicators to initialize. An off-boundary start can produce a partial first bar. Catalog series feed both the strategy's bars and the venue's fills; declared tick and custom series are replayed too, each custom series (`Backtest::custom`, a `Custom:<type name>` entry of the strategy's `[data].series`, such as an LSE reference series) by its data type's identifier over its slices of the window, one over the whole window but on a root, where each contract's series is read over the slice it stands for the root, as the root's other series are (`DataRequirements::custom_slices`), routed to the strategy by its data type, a row stored under the identifier as another data type, of other metadata, failing the run naming both, since Nautilus would publish it under the stored one, each row at the time its values are known: an LSE daily row at its date's end, so that a day's close reaches the strategy at the next day's 00:00 UTC. A custom series of a type with no row of the routing table, the catalog's, written into it from outside helix, is replayed as any other. A custom series the catalog holds nothing of over a slice is warned of as Nautilus' `BacktestNode` warns of any other series it reads nothing of, `No data found for config: DataType(...)`, per contract on a root, as a root's other series are one data config per contract, and the run goes on: no series has a coverage check. A custom type is decoded by its registered name: helix registers every custom type it compiles once as the process starts (`venues::register_custom_data`), and a config naming a type Nautilus has no registration of is refused as it loads. Stored book depth and funding are included where applicable. Roots expand into dated-contract slices; see [rolls](../rolls/README.md) for boundaries and continuous-bar restrictions.

The catalog is not checked for completeness before a run. Missing instruments can fail the node, but missing series may only produce a warning; partial coverage and gaps replay as quiet periods. Stored intervals are request bookkeeping, not proof of completeness. Inspect coverage before trusting results, including the earlier data needed for model dataset warmup. No data for an hour means no bar, not a synthetic flat bar.

Ordinary replay streams data in chunks. A run with custom data loads **all** series for the window at once because Nautilus' streaming node cannot retain the preloaded custom data across chunks. Large depth histories can therefore exhaust memory; reduce the window or data volume.

## Fills and costs

The default fill model adds no slippage. External LAST bars, including IB minute TRADES bars and OKX candles, drive fills without a quoted spread. A market order on a decision bar can fill at the last minute's close; the assumed open-high-low-close sequence determines which resting order fills first within a minute. This is not observed intrabar order and can be optimistic. Minute volume can limit the amount filled at the close, with the remainder one tick worse; thin or zero-volume periods are especially unreliable.

On quotes, buys fill at the ask and sells at the bid of the last quote. Resting orders are checked on each replayed quote, external bar and trade tick (Nautilus' `bar_execution` and `trade_execution`, both on by default), so a crossing between them can be missed. A gap can leave a stale quote in use; without an earlier quote an order is rejected. Quote sizes may be nominal rather than observed depth, and liquidity is not consumed across orders. Tick quotes give more detail than minute bars but do not remove these limitations.

Without `--latency`, an order command reaches the venue as it is sent, so a market order fills on the quote it was sent on. `--latency <duration>`, such as `150ms` (read as the config's durations are), delays each submit, modify and cancel by that much: Nautilus' `StaticLatencyModel` of that base latency, with no extra per kind of command, as Nautilus' guides configure it (`backtest::Latency`). A delayed command is processed at the first settlement point at or after its arrival: a quote or trade of its instrument, after the venue has taken it in, or a timer (Nautilus' `concepts/backtesting/execution-flow.md`, "Latency-delayed commands"). A market order then fills on the first quote of its instrument at or after send plus latency, not on the one it was sent on; between quotes, on the last quote at the next timer. Only the command's way to the venue is delayed: the venue's fills and other events reach the strategy as they occur. One latency applies to every venue of the run; a model strategy's dataset runs are at their config's (`[model] dataset`).

Fixed commission is charged on an order's first fill; a rate and an amount per unit of quantity are charged on every fill, the latter rounded to the currency's precision a fill, a half cent to the even cent, as Nautilus rounds money (`--commission-per-quantity`, Pepperstone's per-lot charges: see [Pepperstone](../venues/pepperstone/README.md#margins-and-costs)). None combines a rate with a minimum, so choose the approximation carefully for IB orders. The commission is the run's one cost source (`backtest::Commission`): the venues' fee model is made from it; a model strategy's dataset runs and targets are at its config's (`[model] dataset`, see [targets](../model/README.md#targets-and-datasets)). Stored OKX funding is settled on open positions; absent funding is not checked or settled. The simulated settlement price and timing can differ from OKX, including orders placed at a funding boundary. See [OKX funding](../venues/okx/README.md#funding-and-margins) for availability.

## Leverage and venues

`Backtest::run` takes each simulated venue's position mode from its broker through `venues::oms_type`: IC Markets and Pepperstone hedge; IB and OKX net. `Backtest::run_dataset` instead sets every venue to hedging so its long and short targets can settle independently. Callers choose the run, not a position mode.

Each venue a broker trades starts with the specified balance unless it is read only for the pairs of `[trade] conversions` (see below). A venue no broker trades, only read, such as `LSEDGE`, starts with nothing in the balance's currency and uses Nautilus' default position mode in a trading run, so that it adds no money: a run reading its series ends with the balances and return statistics of the run without them, its empty account aside, which the report lists. Dataset accounts stay frozen at their starting balances and require no margin (see [dataset runs](../model/README.md#targets-and-datasets)). The same leverage and fee model apply to every venue in a multi-venue run. A trade is sized on the equity converted into its instrument's P&L currency through the pairs of `[trade] conversions` (see [orders](../strategy/README.md#orders-and-positions)): a balance in another currency than a traded instrument's P&L is accepted, on a broker whose account has a base currency (see below), when its currency reaches each traded P&L currency through the pairs, as the catalog defines them, by Nautilus' own exchange-rate path search (`nautilus_common::xrate::get_exchange_rate`, each pair at a rate of 1), and refused before the run otherwise, naming both currencies; without conversions, a balance must be in each traded P&L currency. Fill the pairs with fill-catalog over the backtest's window: the run replays them as the data requirements carry them, a cTrader pair's quotes, an IB pair's 1-minute BID and ASK bars, which its rates are read from; a pair on a venue the run reads nothing else on, IDEALPRO's for GLBX traded through IB, is simulated with its broker's position mode but starts with nothing in the balance's currency, as a venue no broker trades does, so that the run's money and its returns are those of the other venues alone; the report lists its empty account. A pair the strategy also trades, EURUSD on IC traded on a balance in EUR, is one of its instruments: its venue starts with the balance. A simulated venue keeps the account its broker keeps live (`venues::has_base_currency`): a cTrader broker's account, IC Markets' or Pepperstone's, has the balance's currency as its base, as the live account has its deposit currency, so Nautilus books its margins, commissions and P&L in that currency, converting at the rates of the pairs on the traded instrument's venue; IB's and OKX's accounts have none, each currency held apart, and Nautilus books such an account's fills only in a currency it holds: a loss or a commission in a currency the account does not hold is not booked, while a gain is (`AccountsManager::update_balance_multi_currency` in Nautilus' portfolio crate, its branches "intentionally asymmetric until cross-currency equity tracking is implemented", [at the pinned revision](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/blob/97ecbafd6621241a2222ef5a31cf887c6d96d950/crates/portfolio/src/manager.rs#L1143-L1149); no upstream issue tracks it). So a backtest refuses before its run a balance in another currency than the P&L currency of an instrument traded whose broker's account has no base currency (`venues::has_base_currency`), naming the instrument, its venue, both currencies and that limitation, rather than count the trades' wins and lose their losses: gold on GLBX, traded through IB, is backtested on a balance in USD, where its conversions convert nothing. A dataset run, whose account is frozen and books nothing, takes a dataset balance in any currency the pairs reach, and a live run is not refused, its broker booking its account. Dataset runs have no base currency.

Backtests run unleveraged under Nautilus' default margin model, so a position locks its notional times its instrument's margin, which holds the account's: IC and Pepperstone instruments carry the broker's margin, and OKX instruments the account's leverage when their definitions were filled (see [OKX](../venues/okx/README.md#funding-and-margins)). IB instruments have zero margins and lock no margin in this simulation. Orders exceeding free initial margin can be denied, but liquidation is disabled; the simulation does not reproduce venue liquidations.

## Report

PnL is realized and after commissions, including funding; open positions can leave unrealized PnL outside that figure. Costs are derived from fills and the last quote at or before each fill. After the run the report reads the replayed quotes from the catalog again, a batch at a time, so it holds no quote history in memory. Spread is the fill's distance from the quote mid, times quantity and multiplier; with no replayed quotes it is `n/a`, not zero proven spread. Cost per trade divides total commission and measured spread by fills. Gross PnL adds both back to realized PnL, so an open position's opening spread makes it too high. Nautilus nets a commission into a position's PnL only when it is charged in the position's settlement currency; one charged in another currency, such as `--commission "3 EUR"` on a USD instrument, is in no PnL, so the report lists it apart as `Commission outside PnL` and counts it in neither cost per trade nor gross PnL. Inverse instruments are refused by this calculation. Max drawdown is a negative fraction; some statistics can be unavailable with insufficient observations.

## Model retraining

For model strategies, `--models` selects the shared store, and the target-generating dataset runs are at the commission, balance and latency the config declares (`[model] dataset`), not the backtest's: `--commission`, `--balance` and `--latency` are the simulated account's alone, so backtests at any account size, commission or latency trade the one model of the config, and the report names the run's costs (`Run at:`) and those its models were trained at (`Models trained at:`), each with its latency when it has one. The dataset balance is the equity the model's reference stake is of (`[trade] risk` of it: see [reference stake](../model/README.md#reference-stake-and-the-strategys-stake)). A dataset run's account is frozen at that balance and margins nothing (see [dataset runs](../model/README.md#targets-and-datasets)). The backtest's balance and the dataset balance must each reach the traded instrument's P&L currency through the config's conversions, by one check, each refused before any build otherwise, naming its currency, and on a broker whose account has no base currency the backtest's balance must be in that currency, which the dataset balance need not be (see [leverage and venues](#leverage-and-venues)); the dataset balance is converted at each decision's rate (see [reference stake](../model/README.md#reference-stake-and-the-strategys-stake)). A backtest trains nothing: its models are those `train` published, over the span or at its times (see [scheduling](../model/README.md#scheduling)), so that it never trains over the range it judges. Before any replay it refuses a store holding no model set to trade on at its start, none valid by then, as a live start warns of it: it names the retrain time at its start, the last on the grid not after it, or the start itself never retrained, and the `train` command filling it, at that time or over the backtest's span. Otherwise it builds the dataset days of its span missing, for its scores, in its own process, each dataset run a Nautilus node of its own, disposed before the next starts, then runs; a failed dataset build stops the backtest.

Each decision uses the latest valid model set, the models one train published, subject to their checks, as live does: a retrain time the store lacks leaves the model set before it deciding through its period, and a model set failing its checks takes no trade until it loads (see [store](../model/README.md#store-validity-and-versions)). Scores after the report compare out-of-sample predictions and economics against baselines for each side and, where configured, the meta model. Periods without a model or enough observations have unavailable statistics. See [model scoring](../model/README.md#scores) for the metrics and their populations. Backtest models are valid from their scheduled training time, whereas live models become valid only after training finishes, introducing some optimism. See [model guidance](../model/README.md) for datasets, validity and authoring, and [live operations](../live/README.md) for live retraining.
