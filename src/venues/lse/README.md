# LSE

`fill-catalog` fills the history of `LSEDGE` instruments, London Strategic Edge's own derived series, of `GLBX` instruments, CME Globex contracts at Levels 1 to 3, and of `OPRA` instruments, US option contracts, through the [node fill](../../catalog/README.md), each series' request naming the client of the [LSE adapter](../../../crates/lse/README.md). `LSEDGE`'s row of the [routing table](../mod.rs) takes definitions from the catalog alone, history and live data from LSE, and no broker: LSE trades nothing. `GLBX`'s and `OPRA`'s take definitions from [Databento](../databento/README.md), history from LSE, and live data and orders from [IB](../ib/README.md). Set `LSE_API_KEY` for `LSEDGE` and `OPRA`, the free key opening LSE's US options, and `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, a CME Group plan's key, for `GLBX` in the environment or `.env`; each is read only when something is missing. An `LSEDGE` series can be trades, LSE's tick slices, and bars, LSE's candles, from `1-SECOND-LAST-EXTERNAL` to `4-HOUR-LAST-EXTERNAL`, each stamped at its close, as every bar in helix is. Every derived market fills so: US, UK and Canadian stocks, futures and crypto as trades or bars; forex and commodities, of which LSE stores no trades, as bars, such as gold spot (`XAU/USD-COMMODITIES.LSEDGE`); and indices as quotes or bars, an index's ticks being its values, of no size: each is a quote whose bid and ask are the value, with no sizes, as a live run streams an index's ticks, and a declared index's trades fail the fill naming it, an index having none. A bar type of another width, of another price, or daily and longer is refused naming it (see [the adapter's exports](../../../crates/lse/README.md#exports)): LSE's daily, weekly and monthly candles carry no close and are its minute bars aggregated, so declare such a bar `from` a shorter LSE candle (1-MINUTE or 4-HOUR EXTERNAL), and Nautilus aggregates it on the calendar (see [strategy bars](../../strategy/README.md#configuration-and-series)). A `GLBX` contract's series can be trades or quotes, with the sizes LSE sends, book depths, ten levels a side, from LSE's Level 2 book, and book deltas, from its Level 3 orders, each delta event ending `F_LAST` (see [the adapter's book and orders](../../../crates/lse/README.md#book-and-orders)); an `OPRA` contract's, trades, LSE's tick slices of it, and bars, its candles, as an `LSEDGE` series'. Quotes of an `LSEDGE` series but an index, or of an `OPRA` contract, fail with LSE's refusal (`400 data-unavailable`), LSE serving their past as timeframes alone, and any other request fails as unsupported, a `GLBX` contract's bars among them.

An `LSEDGE` instrument is LSE's symbol, a hyphen and its LSE market in capitals: GOLD in `futures` is `GOLD-FUTURES.LSEDGE`, the 10-year Treasury futures `US10Y-FUTURES.LSEDGE`, Apple `AAPL-US_STOCKS.LSEDGE`, Vodafone `VOD-UK_STOCKS.LSEDGE`, the euro `EUR_USD-FOREX.LSEDGE`, bitcoin `BTCUSDT-CRYPTO.LSEDGE`, the S&P 500 `SPX-INDICES.LSEDGE` (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)). LSE publishes no definitions, so write each into the catalog by hand before filling, as an `IndexInstrument` of that id, LSE's symbol as raw symbol, the currency of its prices, a price precision and increment to its tick (gold futures to 0.1, the 10-year note to 1/64, 0.015625, gold spot to 0.01) and whole lots as sizes, stamped at 1970 so that it stands for any time; write it with Nautilus' catalog API (`ParquetDataCatalog::write_instruments`) on the pinned crates. No helix command writes one and the repository keeps none. The data engine answers each definition request from the catalog before asking LSE; a fill of an instrument the catalog does not define fails at its definition request, naming it, before LSE is asked, and a definition coarser than LSE's prices fails the fill rather than round them.

A `GLBX` contract takes the ID Databento gives it, in Nautilus' two-digit year: GCZ6 of 2026 is `GCZ26.GLBX`. LSE names it as CME does, by its year's last digit, a name that comes round every ten years, so the adapter asks LSE for the listing of the contract's month, which it looks up in LSE's catalogue (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)). Its definition is Databento's: the fill asks it of Databento's client, by client id, before the contract's series, over the span of what the catalog lacks of them, and the data engine writes it back to the catalog and puts it in the cache, where the LSE client reads its precision; once the catalog holds it, Nautilus answers from the catalog and Databento is not asked. Set `DATABENTO_API_KEY` too: it is read, and Databento's dataset end checked, whenever a `GLBX` series lacks something. CME Group Lite+ holds twelve years of Level 1 (`history-limit` before them) and no Level 2 or 3: a fill of a `GLBX` contract's book depths or deltas on its key fails with LSE's `level-required`, naming CME Group Standard, the cheapest plan that sells them. A contract's book and orders are asked of LSE an hour an export, as LSE's docs ask of a busy contract. A day of a busy contract's quotes is past the rows one export may read (GCZ6's, about 2.6 million), so LSE is asked for it in halves. LSE stamps a trade at an earlier time than the Databento trades the catalog may hold: GCZ6's of 21 October 2026, the same trades one to one, came 0.17 ms to 347 ms (median 0.29 ms) before Databento's, whose time Nautilus' Databento decoder takes from Databento's receipt (`ts_recv`).

An `OPRA` contract takes the ID Databento gives it, its OSI symbol under `OPRA`: AAPL's 355 call of 20 November 2026 is `AAPL  261120C00355000.OPRA`, its root padded to six characters. LSE's US options list it under that symbol, which names one contract, so the adapter asks LSE by it (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)). Its definition is Databento's, of `OPRA.PILLAR`, asked as a `GLBX` contract's is, with `DATABENTO_API_KEY` and that dataset's end checked; a contract's definition of a day costs about $0.000002. Nautilus' Databento decoder takes an option's multiplier from Databento's record, which for OPRA gives none, so the definition's multiplier is 1, not the 100 shares of a standard contract (`decode_option_multiplier`), and its tick a cent, Databento giving none either; a trade LSE prices finer than the definition fails the fill rather than being rounded. LSE stores US options since October 2025, their trades alone, as OPRA reports them, with no side. LSE's greeks of an option (`data=greeks`, one row a traded minute) are not filled: their history waits for Nautilus to add a historical request of its `OptionGreeks`. At the pinned Nautilus, `DataResponse` has no variant of them and the data engine's catalog write-back (`response_batches` in `crates/data/src/engine/streaming.rs`) writes none back, so the node fill cannot ask for them (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)).

Requests are split into day-long windows from the request start, as Databento's were; the adapter asks LSE a UTC day an export within each, one download at a time on each key, within the calls a minute of the free plan, or of CME Group Lite on the CME key, unless configured otherwise, and asks again in halves for an export LSE refuses as too wide (past its 2,500,000 rows an export). LSE's refusal, such as `symbol-unknown` for a symbol it does not list or `plan-required`, fails the fill with its code, message and required plan; a quota (`rows-exhausted`, the free plan's 12.5 million rows a day, or `allowance-exhausted`, its 12.5 GiB a week) stops it with LSE's message, the windows filled before it kept, and the next run asks for what is still missing. LSE stores a UTC day the morning after it: a window reaching into the current UTC day, or into the day before while LSE answers no rows of it, is answered but not written back, the span logged, and the next run asks for it again. A window ending at the current day's midnight holds its first instant, so end a fill at the last instant of the day before (`…T23:59:59.999999999Z`) to have that day written back once LSE has loaded it. See the [adapter's exports](../../../crates/lse/README.md#exports) for retries, checksums and conversion, and [shared fill semantics](../../catalog/README.md) for coverage and resume.

LSE's reference series are custom data of no instrument, `LseRow` (see [the adapter's reference rows](../../../crates/lse/README.md#reference-rows)): the rows of each of its 21 reference collections, of its macro series of releases and of yields, and of an instrument's splits and dividends, its factors. A strategy's config lists each in `[data].custom` by its type and metadata, `{ data = "Custom:LseRow", metadata = { source = "bond_yields", symbol = "US10Y" } }` for the US 10-year yield of the daily bond yields, `source = "economic_calendar", symbol = "US.Core Inflation Rate YoY"` for US core inflation's releases, `source = "macro_release"` or `"macro_yield"` with a macro series' code, `source = "factors_dividends"` or `"factors_splits", symbol = "AAPL-US_STOCKS"` for an instrument's, its LSE symbol and market as an `LSEDGE` id writes them; data requirements carry it as its Nautilus data type (`DataRequirements::custom`), keyed in the catalog by its identifier, `US10Y-BOND_YIELDS`. They need no definition and no venue: `fill-catalog` asks LSE's client for each over the fill's window, whole, and Nautilus writes the rows back as custom data under that identifier; custom data of any other type of no instrument fails the fill before anything connects, and so do LSE's macro observations (`macro_observation`), refused by LSE's client before LSE is asked: an observation is keyed by the day of its period, not when it came out, LSE giving no release time of it, so declare its releases (`macro_release`) instead. Each row is stamped when its values are known: a day's close or market values, a filing's day, an ex-date and a report date at its date's end; an interval at its close; a snapshot at its update; a release at the time it came out (the economic calendar's `datetime`, a macro release's `released_at`); a COT report at its release date's end, or at LSE's ingest of it where LSE gives no release date; a financial report at the SEC's acceptance of its period's statements. So a window holds the rows known in it, and a series whose rows are published long after their key (COT, financial reports) is asked of LSE whole up to the window's end. The earnings calendar's figures are as LSE holds them today, split-adjusted after the fact: their ratios hold, their levels do not. LSE stores a date the morning after it, as it does a market's day: a window whose last date LSE may not have loaded yet is answered but not written back, the span logged, and the next run asks for it again; so is a window of releases holding one, past, LSE has not filled with its actual. A backtest replays the rows by that data type, registering LSE's type itself. A live run reading any is refused before anything connects: the adapter streams none. data-ws lists four US "bond yields" (`USB02Y/USD` to `USB30Y/USD`), but they tick the notes' prices, not yields, and no market of LSE's HTTP API lists them, so none is streamed (see the [adapter's live quotes](../../../crates/lse/README.md#live-quotes)).

A live run streams the quotes of the `LSEDGE` instruments it reads from LSE's sockets, bid and ask with no sizes (see the [adapter's live quotes](../../../crates/lse/README.md#live-quotes)), through LSE's data client alone, routed `LSEDGE`: no execution client, LSE trading nothing, and IB keeps the default route. Set `LSE_API_KEY`; the node reads it when it creates the client, and the client connects to the socket of each instrument's market at the node's start, under that socket's name of it (data-ws for US stocks, forex, commodities, futures and indices, london-ws for UK stocks and crypto, tsx-ws for Canadian stocks), failing it, as Nautilus fails a node whose client does not connect, if LSE refuses the key or a second connection, if the socket does not list an instrument, or if a socket's instruments are more than one connection of the key may stream (16 a socket on the free key). tsx-ws lists one Canadian stock, `NVDA.TO`, of those it streams, so another fails the start as unlisted. The definitions come from the catalog through the warmup, which asks each one the cache lacks of LSE's client before anything else; the data engine answers it from the catalog, as a fill's, and the client reads it before it subscribes. One the catalog lacks reaches LSE's client, which fails the request naming the instrument, ending the warmup incomplete with that reason, and the instrument's subscription then fails naming it too. LSE has no history of a derived market's quotes but an index's, so the warmup of another `LSEDGE` quote series ends incomplete, logging LSE's refusal (`400 data-unavailable`), and the series' features warm on the live quotes. `GLBX`'s and `OPRA`'s live data stay IB's: the adapter streams neither CME data nor options yet. A backtest simulates `LSEDGE` as a venue no broker trades: it starts with no money and keeps positions as Nautilus' venue default (see [backtest venues](../../backtest/README.md#leverage-and-venues)).

## Tests

Offline tests of the fill through the node against the adapter's mock LSE peer, a series of each derived market among them, of `GLBX` fills against it and a mock Databento peer, of a contract's trades and quotes and, from LSE's documented book and orders, of its book depths and deltas, and of an `OPRA` fill against them, of a contract's trades and bars, are in `src/venues/lse`, the live clients' routing in `src/venues/mod.rs`, a live warmup serving an `LSEDGE` definition from the catalog before LSE's client streams the series, against the adapter's mock socket, in `tests/node_warmup`, a backtest reading a filled `LSEDGE` series beside IC's trade in `tests/backtest/backtest_read_only_venue.rs`, and one replaying filled bond yields to the strategy in `tests/backtest/backtest_custom_series.rs`; the adapter's own are in [the crate](../../../crates/lse/README.md#tests). The fill tests write back a series of each kind of LSE's other sources, the economic calendar, a macro series of releases, the Commitments of Traders and an instrument's dividends, each row at the time it is known. The gated tests `tests/live/lse_history.rs` fill one day of gold and 10-year Treasury futures trades, one day of gold spot candles as 1-minute bars, one day of the daily 10-year US bond yield, US core inflation's release of October 2026 in the economic calendar and the macro releases, three days of the 10-year macro yield, ZN's Commitments of Traders of September 2025 and of September 2026, AAPL's financial reports of the first half of 2026 and its dividends and splits, five minutes of the S&P 500's values as quotes, one day of GCZ26.GLBX's trades and quotes, and one day of `AAPL  261120C00355000.OPRA`'s trades and 1-minute bars, each contract's definition from Databento, from LSE into a temporary catalog, with the keys of the environment or `.env` (`LSE_API_KEY`, `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, `DATABENTO_API_KEY`); they run only with `NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1` set in the shell and count against the free key's rows a day; none fills a book or orders, helix's CME key holding Level 1 alone:

```sh
NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1 cargo test --test live lse_history:: -- --nocapture
```

The gated test `tests/live/lse_quotes.rs` streams the gold futures quotes from LSE for a minute through LSE's client on a node, the definition in a temporary catalog; data-ws allows the key one connection, so no live run may hold it meanwhile:

```sh
NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1 cargo test --test live lse_quotes:: -- --nocapture
```

The gated test `tests/live/lse_markets.rs` streams the quotes of a series of each derived market from LSE through LSE's client on a node, each on its socket: gold futures, Apple, the euro, gold spot and the S&P 500 on data-ws, bitcoin and Vodafone on london-ws, Nvidia's Canadian listing on tsx-ws. LSE opens each subscription with the symbol's last tick, so it stops once each has quoted, within a minute; bitcoin's quote must be of the last hour, as data-ws, which lists it too, would send one of 8 November. It holds one connection on each of the three sockets meanwhile:

```sh
NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1 cargo test --test live lse_markets:: -- --nocapture
```

Run on 13 November 2026 at 22:35 UTC with the free key, every series quoted within a second of the subscription, bitcoin's live.
