# LSE

`fill-catalog` fills the history of `LSEDGE` instruments, London Strategic Edge's own derived series, of `GLBX` instruments, CME Globex contracts at Level 1, and of `OPRA` instruments, US option contracts, through the [node fill](../../catalog/README.md), each series' request naming the client of the [LSE adapter](../../../crates/lse/README.md). `LSEDGE`'s row of the [routing table](../mod.rs) takes definitions from the catalog alone, history and live data from LSE, and no broker: LSE trades nothing. `GLBX`'s and `OPRA`'s take definitions from [Databento](../databento/README.md), history from LSE, and live data and orders from [IB](../ib/README.md). Set `LSE_API_KEY` for `LSEDGE` and `OPRA`, the free key opening LSE's US options, and `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, a CME Group plan's key, for `GLBX` in the environment or `.env`; each is read only when something is missing. An `LSEDGE` series can be trades, LSE's tick slices, and bars, LSE's candles, from `1-SECOND-LAST-EXTERNAL` to `4-HOUR-LAST-EXTERNAL`, each stamped at its close, as every bar in helix is; a series LSE serves only as candles, such as gold spot (`XAU/USD-COMMODITIES.LSEDGE`), is filled as bars. A bar type of another width, of another price, or daily and longer is refused naming it (see [the adapter's exports](../../../crates/lse/README.md#exports)): LSE's daily, weekly and monthly candles carry no close and are its minute bars aggregated, so declare such a bar `from` a shorter LSE candle (1-MINUTE or 4-HOUR EXTERNAL), and Nautilus aggregates it on the calendar (see [strategy bars](../../strategy/README.md#configuration-and-series)). A `GLBX` contract's series can be trades or quotes, with the sizes LSE sends; an `OPRA` contract's, trades, LSE's tick slices of it, and bars, its candles, as an `LSEDGE` series'. Quotes of an `LSEDGE` series or an `OPRA` contract fail with LSE's refusal (`400 data-unavailable`), LSE serving their past as timeframes alone, and any other request fails as unsupported, a `GLBX` contract's bars, book depths and deltas among them.

An `LSEDGE` instrument is LSE's symbol, a hyphen and its LSE market in capitals: GOLD in `futures` is `GOLD-FUTURES.LSEDGE`, the 10-year Treasury futures `US10Y-FUTURES.LSEDGE` (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)). LSE publishes no definitions, so write each into the catalog by hand before filling, as an `IndexInstrument` of that id, LSE's symbol as raw symbol, the currency of its prices, a price precision and increment to its tick (gold futures to 0.1, the 10-year note to 1/64, 0.015625, gold spot to 0.01) and whole lots as sizes, stamped at 1970 so that it stands for any time; write it with Nautilus' catalog API (`ParquetDataCatalog::write_instruments`) on the pinned crates. No helix command writes one and the repository keeps none. The data engine answers each definition request from the catalog before asking LSE; a fill of an instrument the catalog does not define fails at its definition request, naming it, before LSE is asked, and a definition coarser than LSE's prices fails the fill rather than round them.

A `GLBX` contract takes the ID Databento gives it, in Nautilus' two-digit year: GCZ6 of 2026 is `GCZ26.GLBX`. LSE names it as CME does, by its year's last digit, a name that comes round every ten years, so the adapter asks LSE for the listing of the contract's month, which it looks up in LSE's catalogue (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)). Its definition is Databento's: the fill asks it of Databento's client, by client id, before the contract's series, over the span of what the catalog lacks of them, and the data engine writes it back to the catalog and puts it in the cache, where the LSE client reads its precision; once the catalog holds it, Nautilus answers from the catalog and Databento is not asked. Set `DATABENTO_API_KEY` too: it is read, and Databento's dataset end checked, whenever a `GLBX` series lacks something. CME Group Lite+ holds twelve years of Level 1 (`history-limit` before them). A day of a busy contract's quotes is past the rows one export may read (GCZ6's, about 2.6 million), so LSE is asked for it in halves. LSE stamps a trade at an earlier time than the Databento trades the catalog may hold: GCZ6's of 21 October 2026, the same trades one to one, came 0.17 ms to 347 ms (median 0.29 ms) before Databento's, whose time Nautilus' Databento decoder takes from Databento's receipt (`ts_recv`).

An `OPRA` contract takes the ID Databento gives it, its OSI symbol under `OPRA`: AAPL's 355 call of 20 November 2026 is `AAPL  261120C00355000.OPRA`, its root padded to six characters. LSE's US options list it under that symbol, which names one contract, so the adapter asks LSE by it (see [the adapter's instruments](../../../crates/lse/README.md#instruments)). Its definition is Databento's, of `OPRA.PILLAR`, asked as a `GLBX` contract's is, with `DATABENTO_API_KEY` and that dataset's end checked; a contract's definition of a day costs about $0.000002. Nautilus' Databento decoder takes an option's multiplier from Databento's record, which for OPRA gives none, so the definition's multiplier is 1, not the 100 shares of a standard contract (`decode_option_multiplier`), and its tick a cent, Databento giving none either; a trade LSE prices finer than the definition fails the fill rather than being rounded. LSE stores US options since October 2025, their trades alone, as OPRA reports them, with no side. LSE's greeks of an option (`data=greeks`, one row a traded minute) are not filled: Nautilus' option greeks have no historical request in its data engine, so the node fill cannot ask for them.

Requests are split into day-long windows from the request start, as Databento's were; the adapter asks LSE a UTC day an export within each, one download at a time on each key, within the calls a minute of the free plan, or of CME Group Lite on the CME key, unless configured otherwise, and asks again in halves for an export LSE refuses as too wide (past its 2,500,000 rows an export). LSE's refusal, such as `symbol-unknown` for a symbol it does not list or `plan-required`, fails the fill with its code, message and required plan; a quota (`rows-exhausted`, the free plan's 12.5 million rows a day, or `allowance-exhausted`, its 12.5 GiB a week) stops it with LSE's message, the windows filled before it kept, and the next run asks for what is still missing. LSE stores a UTC day the morning after it: a window reaching into the current UTC day, or into the day before while LSE answers no rows of it, is answered but not written back, the span logged, and the next run asks for it again. A window ending at the current day's midnight holds its first instant, so end a fill at the last instant of the day before (`…T23:59:59.999999999Z`) to have that day written back once LSE has loaded it. See the [adapter's exports](../../../crates/lse/README.md#exports) for retries, checksums and conversion, and [shared fill semantics](../../catalog/README.md) for coverage and resume.

LSE's reference series, the rows of its reference collections, are custom data of no instrument, `LseRow` (see [the adapter's reference rows](../../../crates/lse/README.md#reference-rows)): a strategy's config lists each in `[data].custom` by its type and metadata, `{ data = "Custom:LseRow", metadata = { source = "bond_yields", symbol = "US10Y" } }` for the US 10-year yield of the daily bond yields, and data requirements carry it as its Nautilus data type (`DataRequirements::custom`), keyed in the catalog by its identifier, `US10Y-BOND_YIELDS`. They need no definition and no venue: `fill-catalog` asks LSE's client for each over the fill's window, whole, and Nautilus writes the rows back as custom data under that identifier; custom data of any other type of no instrument fails the fill before anything connects. Each row is stamped when its values are known, a date's at its end, an interval's at its close, so a window holds the rows known in it. LSE stores a date the morning after it, as it does a market's day: a window whose last date LSE may not have loaded yet is answered but not written back, the span logged, and the next run asks for it again. A backtest replays the rows by that data type, registering LSE's type itself. A live run reading any is refused before anything connects: the adapter streams none yet.

A live run streams the quotes of the `LSEDGE` instruments it reads from LSE's sockets, bid and ask with no sizes (see the [adapter's live quotes](../../../crates/lse/README.md#live-quotes)), through LSE's data client alone, routed `LSEDGE`: no execution client, LSE trading nothing, and IB keeps the default route. Set `LSE_API_KEY`; the node reads it when it creates the client, and the client connects to the socket of each instrument's market at the node's start, failing it, as Nautilus fails a node whose client does not connect, if LSE refuses the key or a second connection, if the socket does not list an instrument, or if they are more than one connection of the key may stream (16 on the free key). The definitions come from the catalog through the warmup, which asks each one the cache lacks of LSE's client before anything else; the data engine answers it from the catalog, as a fill's, and the client reads it before it subscribes. One the catalog lacks reaches LSE's client, which fails the request naming the instrument, ending the warmup incomplete with that reason, and the instrument's subscription then fails naming it too. LSE has no history of a derived market's quotes, so the warmup of an `LSEDGE` quote series ends incomplete, logging LSE's refusal (`400 data-unavailable`), and the series' features warm on the live quotes. `GLBX`'s and `OPRA`'s live data stay IB's: the adapter streams neither CME data nor options yet. A backtest simulates `LSEDGE` as a venue no broker trades: it starts with no money and keeps positions as Nautilus' venue default (see [backtest venues](../../backtest/README.md#leverage-and-venues)).

## Tests

Offline tests of the fill through the node against the adapter's mock LSE peer, and of `GLBX` and `OPRA` fills against it and a mock Databento peer, are in `src/venues/lse`, the live clients' routing in `src/venues/mod.rs`, a live warmup serving an `LSEDGE` definition from the catalog before LSE's client streams the series, against the adapter's mock socket, in `tests/node_warmup`, a backtest reading a filled `LSEDGE` series beside IC's trade in `tests/backtest/backtest_read_only_venue.rs`, and one replaying filled bond yields to the strategy in `tests/backtest/backtest_custom_series.rs`; the adapter's own are in [the crate](../../../crates/lse/README.md#tests). The gated tests `tests/live/lse_history.rs` fill one day of gold and 10-year Treasury futures trades, one day of gold spot candles as 1-minute bars, one day of the daily 10-year US bond yield, one day of GCZ26.GLBX's trades and quotes, and one day of `AAPL  261120C00355000.OPRA`'s trades and 1-minute bars, each contract's definition from Databento, from LSE into a temporary catalog, with the keys of the environment or `.env` (`LSE_API_KEY`, `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, `DATABENTO_API_KEY`); they run only with `NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1` set in the shell and count against the free key's rows a day:

```sh
NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1 cargo test --test live lse_history:: -- --nocapture
```

The gated test `tests/live/lse_quotes.rs` streams the gold futures quotes from LSE for a minute through LSE's client on a node, the definition in a temporary catalog; data-ws allows the key one connection, so no live run may hold it meanwhile:

```sh
NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1 cargo test --test live lse_quotes:: -- --nocapture
```
