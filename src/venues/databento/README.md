# Databento definitions

`fill-catalog` fills the definitions of `GLBX` instruments from Databento's `GLBX.MDP3` dataset, of `XNAS` instruments, Nasdaq TotalView stocks, from its `XNAS.ITCH`, and of `OPRA` instruments, US option contracts, from its `OPRA.PILLAR`, through the [node fill](../../catalog/README.md), each request naming Databento's client, Nautilus' data engine writing each back to the catalog; their history is [LSE](../lse/README.md)'s, and [IB](../ib/README.md) serves their live data and trades them (see the [routing table](../mod.rs)). A contract's definition is asked in LSE's fill of its series, before them, over the span of what the catalog lacks of them; once the catalog holds it, Nautilus answers later requests from it and Databento is not asked. Set `DATABENTO_API_KEY` in the environment or `.env`; it is read, and the dataset's end read, whenever a `GLBX`, `XNAS` or `OPRA` series lacks something.

Before starting the node, the fill reads the available end of each dataset it asks, the one Nautilus' client asks a venue's instruments of (its publishers' map: `GLBX.MDP3` for `GLBX`, `XNAS.ITCH` for `XNAS`, the first of the venue's publishers there, `OPRA.PILLAR` for `OPRA`). A dataset lags real time, and Databento refuses a request past its end, so a definition whose window reaches past it, as a fill ending today does, is asked up to it, holding at least the last whole UTC day before it: Databento's historical API gives a snapshot of every definition active at each UTC midnight, Monday to Friday ("any request for definitions for a 24-hour period beginning at UTC midnight is guaranteed to include definitions for all instruments active during that time period", Databento's docs, "Instrument definitions"), and a definition holds over its contract's life. The series of that window are LSE's, whose tail LSE's client answers unwritten until LSE has loaded it.

Dated contracts are stored under their own IDs, in Nautilus' two-digit year (`GCZ26.GLBX`), Databento's default and LSE's; rolled roots fill their dated contracts over their respective slices, not the root itself. An option contract is stored under its OSI symbol, padded, under `OPRA` (`AAPL  261120C00355000.OPRA`), the venue of the `OPRA.PILLAR` publisher that sends its definitions; Databento's OPRA record gives no multiplier, so Nautilus' decoder makes the definition's 1 (`decode_option_multiplier`), not the 100 shares of a standard contract, and its tick a cent, the record giving none either. A stock is stored under its ticker under `XNAS` (`NVDA.XNAS`), the venue of the `XNAS.ITCH` publisher, in Nasdaq's convention, a share class after a dot (`BRK.B.XNAS`), as an `Equity` in round lots, Databento's `min_lot_size_round_lot` (NVDA's 100); Databento sends a stock's definition more than once a day (NVDA's three records of 12 November 2026, one of lots of 1), and Nautilus' Databento client answers with the last of the window asked, and fails a window holding none (`Instrument not found`). Backtest and live IDs must be chosen deliberately: Databento's default venue differs from IB's; see [instrument identity](../../catalog/README.md).

Offline tests cover a definition's window within the dataset's end and the requests Databento takes in `src/venues/databento`; `GLBX`, `XNAS` and `OPRA` fills against mock Databento and LSE peers, Databento's peer replaying its captured definitions of GCZ6, of NVDA and of AAPL's 355 call of 20 November 2026 (`testdata/`, see `testing.rs`), are in `src/venues/lse`.
