# Interactive Brokers

IB serves every venue without a row of its own in the [routing table](../mod.rs), definitions, history and live data, and is its broker; it also trades GLBX's, XNAS's and OPRA's instruments, XNAS's on IB's `NASDAQ`, as Nautilus' IB adapter maps the venue, its clients loading those a live run reads and giving their definitions live, while their definitions in the catalog are [Databento](../databento/README.md)'s and their history and live data [LSE](../lse/README.md)'s. On the live node IB's data client is routed the venues whose live data is IB's, and its execution client takes every order no other broker's takes, GLBX's, XNAS's and OPRA's among them. Helix makes IB's data client not the default route: Nautilus' data engine makes the first data client on no venue it registers the default itself (`DataEngine::register_client`, `crates/data/src/engine/registry.rs:44`) and refuses another made the default after it (`set_default_client`, `registry.rs:101`), and the node builder registers its clients in its `HashMap`'s order (`crates/live/src/node/builder.rs:94`, `:642`), so with LSE's and IB's data clients, both on no venue, making IB's the default failed the build whenever LSE's came first. Nautilus makes one of them the default, in no set order, and since every venue a run reads has a route, nothing depends on which (see [live](../../live/README.md#startup-and-routing)). See [catalog](../../catalog/README.md) for shared fill semantics and [live](../../live/README.md) for node startup, routing, Redis and recovery.

## History

IB's history serves what Nautilus' IB clients request of it (`venues::serves`, `ib::serves`), which fill-catalog fills through the direct writer and a live warmup requests: its own (`EXTERNAL`) time bars of every size IB has, 1, 5, 10, 15 and 30 seconds, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20 and 30 minutes, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 8 hours, a day, a week and a month (Nautilus' `bar_type_to_ib_bar_size`), priced LAST, from IB TRADES, BID and ASK, from IB's BID and ASK, or MID, from IB's MIDPOINT (Nautilus' `price_type_to_ib_what_to_show`); an instrument's quotes, IB's historical BID_ASK ticks; and its trades, IB's TRADES ticks. Nautilus' IB clients ask TRADES for any other price, so no other is served, and request no book. A series it does not serve fails a fill before anything connects and gets no warmup. Some instruments, including IDEALPRO forex and spot metals, have no TRADES: an IDEALPRO pair such as `EUR/USD.IDEALPRO` is filled as its BID and ASK bars, which carry no volume (IB gives none, and Nautilus' IB client stores zero), or as its quotes, and its LAST bars, external or built from its trades, are not served. Nor are the trades of any instrument Nautilus' IB client defines as a currency pair, IDEALPRO's forex and PAXOS's and ZEROHASH's crypto: its `request_trades` refuses them by logging an error and answering nothing, which would leave a warmup asking for them unfinished, so `serves` refuses them by venue, before any definition is known.

Bars are asked as the history client walks back from the end (see below), each request over the longest duration IB's step-size table admits the bars' size in (TWS API, "Historical Data Limitations"; `ib::request_duration`): `1800 S` of 1-second bars up to a year of daily, weekly and monthly bars, a day of 1-minute bars asked as `86400 S`, as `D` counts whole sessions. IB paces bars of 30 seconds or less hard and Nautilus' clients pace nothing, so the writer holds each request of them until IB takes it: none identical within 15 seconds, fewer than six of one contract and price within two seconds, at most 60 within ten minutes (Nautilus asks BID and ASK bars apart, never IB's BID_ASK, which IB counts twice). IB holds no such bars older than six months: a fill of them reaching earlier fails naming that, before IB connects, rather than storing its older windows empty; a warmup reaching earlier gets nothing there. Quotes and trades are asked through Nautilus' `request_ticks`, which pages back from the end 1,000 ticks a request, a trading session at most, and keeps the latest up to a limit the writer sets to bound nothing; IB keeps three years of them, stamps them to the second and documents no pacing of them, so a long fill of a liquid instrument takes many requests, and Nautilus logs and skips a page's errors rather than failing on them.

History starts or reuses the Docker paper gateway, even for a live account. Set `TWS_USERNAME` and `TWS_PASSWORD`; the exchange's Level 1 real-time subscription with Market Data API access must be shared with the paper account, even for historical requests. The fill uses a separate client ID so it can run alongside a live node.

The requested end must be at least one minute before now, checked before starting the gateway: IB can return an in-progress bar, which would otherwise be stored and skipped on resume. IB gives bar opens; the adapter stamps minute bars at their close (`ts_event` and `ts_init`). Full-session data is used for consistency with live; partial session bars can still have misleading close timestamps at coarser cadences.

The history client walks backwards with overlapping explicit-duration requests, each one IB answers within the request timeout: a request by start over the whole range, which Nautilus' IB client sends as one request between its bounds (nautilus_trader#5621), took 1,164 s for a year of minute bars against the 60 s timeout (measured 2026-11-08). Expired futures are resolved using explicit contracts with `includeExpired`; availability is limited and varies, so verify depth for the instrument. IB can return flat zero-volume bars after expiry. Its continuous future is not a substitute for per-contract history: its roll and adjustment differ and it is not directly tradable.

IB's simplified IDs use the contract exchange as venue. Configure MIC mappings explicitly when matching another source; automatic exchange-to-MIC conversion can be nondeterministic where an exchange has multiple MICs. See [instrument identity](../../catalog/README.md).

## Account and gateway

Set a bare account ID with `--account` or `NAUTILUS_IB_ACCOUNT_ID`. A paper ID beginning with `D` selects the paper gateway; another selects live. Set `TWS_USERNAME` and `TWS_PASSWORD` in the environment or `.env`. Live login requires IBKR Mobile 2FA approval within the startup timeout.

`live` starts or reuses the Dockerized gateway; it does not use a manually started gateway or TWS. Pull the image first:

```sh
docker pull ghcr.io/gnzsnz/ib-gateway:stable
```

The paper and live containers use ports 4002 and 4001 respectively and remain running for reuse. The gateway login takes over an existing session of the same IB user; close any manual gateway occupying the port. Concurrent commands share the container, each waiting for its login, but the adapter replaces a container it finds created and not yet started, which can fail the command starting it at that moment: start the second command once the first's container is running. Distinct processes need distinct client IDs. The gateway API is not read-only because even its initial execution connection requires write access. After a gateway restart, periodic checks run but the node does not repeat full startup reconciliation.

## Trading and warmup

IB is netting. Its execution adapter denies reduce-only and post-only orders, so a trade on an instrument IB trades, GLBX's, XNAS's and OPRA's included, sends its bracket's exits neither reduce-only nor post-only and its time close not reduce-only, in a backtest as live (`strategy::orders`).

A trade on IB is sized on the equity IB reports: the account's balance totals alone, not Nautilus' `Portfolio::equity` (`venues::equity_source`). IB reports its account summary in the account's base currency alone, and Nautilus' IB adapter takes its `NetLiquidation`, the account's cash and the value of its positions, as the balance's total (`execution/account.rs`, `AccountSummaryBalance`), so the total holds the open positions' P&L. `Portfolio::equity` adds a reported margin account's open P&L to its balance totals, as Nautilus documents it, so on IB it counts that P&L twice: a trade sent while another position is open on the account would be sized on it twice, in any currency. Nautilus will not change this: nautilus_trader#3565 (IB's net liquidation as the total) and #5634 (the same for Hyperliquid) were closed as not planned, a reported account's equity allowed to differ from the venue's. The cTrader brokers' balances and OKX's cash leave open positions out, so their equity is `Portfolio::equity`. A backtest's simulated IB account is Nautilus' own, its balance calculated from its fills with no open P&L in it (`Account::calculated_account_state`), so it is read from `Portfolio::equity`, which comes to what IB's net liquidation would be. The gated tests `ib_gateway::ib_reports_its_account_summary_in_the_accounts_base_currency_alone` and `ib_gateway::net_liquidation_moves_with_an_open_positions_pnl` pin the two facts this rests on.

A bracket's two exits share an IB OCA group, so IB itself acts on a fill of one, whether the node is up or not. Nautilus' IB adapter sends an order list's exits in no group of its own and takes one from an order's `IBOrderTags` tag (Nautilus' IB guide, "Commands and order attributes"; IB's TWS guide says TWS groups a bracket's children itself, which the explicit group does not rely on). Each exit carries, besides Nautilus' `TAKE_PROFIT` or `STOP_LOSS`, `IBOrderTags:{"ocaGroup":"<entry's client order id>","ocaType":2}` (`venues::bracket_exits_tag`). Type 2, reduce with block, is Nautilus' one-updates-the-other: a partial fill of one exit reduces the other by it and a whole fill cancels it; the adapter reports the reduced quantity as an order update and reads the group back as one-updates-the-other at reconciliation. With block, IB routes one order of the group at a time, so both exits cannot fill; type 3 drops that protection, and type 1 cancels the other exit at a partial fill, leaving the rest of the position unprotected. The group's name is the entry's client order id, unique to the bracket as IB requires (a name once used names no other group) and kept on the exits' tags in the cache across restarts. A backtest and a dataset run send the tag too, and the simulated venue ignores it.

**Such exits lose the reduce-only guarantee**, as Nautilus' IB guide warns: IB cancels no exit when another order closes the position, so an exit filling after that opens a position the other way, which no trade's exits or time close end. The time close cancels the trade's exits and closes its position without waiting for the cancels, so an exit filling before its cancel lands leaves such a position, opened by the close or the exit, whichever fills second. IB cancels the rest of an OCA group when the client cancels one of its orders (IBKR's glossary, "One-Cancels-All (OCA) Order"), so cancelling one exit cancels the other at IB too; the time close's own cancel of the other can then find it cancelled already, which IB refuses (161 or 10148) and the adapter reports as a cancel rejected before it checks the order: a warning, the exit cancelled either way. On the netting account a trade's time close, or an entry on the other side, also closes the other trades in the position, whose exits stay working, each bracket's group its own: the EMA crossover, whose trades overlap, meets this; a model strategy's trades do not overlap.

Live bars use full-session IB minute TRADES bars, matching history; an IDEALPRO pair of `[trade] conversions` is read as its 1-minute BID and ASK bars, the first form of a pair IB serves (`venues::rate_series`), requested at the start over the strategy's warmup, building nothing, then subscribed live from where the warmup ends, its rate read from them (see [live](../../live/README.md#fx-conversions)). A warmup requests an IB instrument's quotes and trades of its requirements too, a traded instrument's quotes no feature reads among them: over a long warmup of a liquid instrument those take IB many requests of 1,000 ticks. Market orders can be priced from cached LAST bars; MID bars alone do not supply that price. Warmup ends at the request time, excluding the partial minute; the live subscription starts there. The data client does not write its historical answer back to the catalog.

Warmup asks in windows under a day: IB's client asks each as one request between its bounds (nautilus_trader#5621), which goes to IB in seconds, where a day's window would go as `3 D`, IB counting days in sessions, three fetched to keep one day's bars. It holds every bar of IB's history over them. IB's client reports a failed history request as an answer with what it got, so a window failing leaves a gap in the warmup with no failure. See [history](#history) for history limitations.

## Tests

Offline tests are in `src/venues/ib`. IB end-to-end tests in `tests/live/` require `NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1` in the shell and a paper `NAUTILUS_IB_ACCOUNT_ID`; they start or reuse the Docker paper gateway. `ib_restart` and `ib_gateway::net_liquidation_moves_with_an_open_positions_pnl` also require `NAUTILUS_IB_LIVE_ORDERS=1` and **place paper-account orders**; ensure the account and market are suitable and check the account afterwards. The latter holds 50 SXR8 for up to 12 minutes while Xetra trades, on a paper account in EUR holding no position. Run live-node tests in separate processes.

```sh
NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1 cargo test --test live ib_gateway::
NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1 NAUTILUS_IB_LIVE_ORDERS=1 cargo test --test live ib_gateway::net_liquidation -- --nocapture
NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1 cargo test --test live ib_minute_bars:: -- --nocapture
NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1 cargo test --test live ib_warmup_days:: -- --nocapture
NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1 cargo test --test live ib_quote_fill:: -- --nocapture
NAUTILUS_IB_RUN=1 NAUTILUS_IB_LIVE_ORDERS=1 cargo test --test live ib_restart:: -- --test-threads=1
```
