API documentation
LSE Data 3.0 is one HTTPS service. Every call is a GET under /v3 with your key in the X-API-Key header. Answers are JSON, except files, which are CSV or Parquet, and pictures, which are WebP. The examples on this page are real answers from the server on 25 October 2026, shortened.
https://download.londonstrategicedge.com/v3/<call>?<parameters>
Get your key on the /data#api | Databank. Live prices stream over WebSockets with the same key: /websocket-documentation | WebSocket documentation.
requests
How a request works
address
The key
Send your key in the X-API-Key header on every call. The server also accepts it as ?key= in the address; use the header, so the key never sits in a URL. A key made on the Databank works within a minute.
curl -H "X-API-Key: $LSE_KEY" "https://download.londonstrategicedge.com/v3/usage"
Answer | When
401 key-missing | No key in the header or the address.
401 key-unknown | A key the server does not know.
403 plan-ended | The key's subscription has ended. It still reads the catalogue and its own `/v3/usage`; every download is refused with the reason.
403 key-withheld | The account holds more keys than its plan allows.
calls
Calls a minute
Every call with a known key counts against its plan, including calls answered with a refusal. One call more answers 429 calls-exhausted, with a Retry-After header and retry_after in the body, both in seconds. /v3/usage shows your figure as calls_per_minute.
Plan | Calls a minute
Free | 60
Global Markets Standard | 500
Global Markets Standard+ | 1,000
Global Markets Ultimate | 5,000
CME Group and Nasdaq: Lite, Lite+, Standard, Standard+, Ultimate | 200, 300, 500, 1,000, 5,000
GET /v3/health          (the 61st call of a minute on the free plan)
429  Retry-After: 57
{"code": "calls-exhausted", "message": "Your plan allows 60 API calls a minute; try again in 57 s", "retry_after": 57}
plans
What any key may read, and what needs a plan
What | Who may read it
The catalogue: /v3/markets, /v3/pairs, /v3/catalogue, /v3/factors, /v3/tables, /v3/overview, /v3/photo, the macro and reference lists | Any key
Previews and estimates of candles and Greeks on the nine included markets, for any dates | Any key
A paid market's figures: tick counts, stored rows, sizes, history bounds and estimates | Any key
Exports, the dataset builder, the options calls, macro and reference rows | A key that belongs to an account
A paid market's rows, its candles included: CME Globex, Nasdaq TotalView | A key on that market's plan, from Lite
Level 2 book and Level 3 orders | A tier that sells the level, on a market that serves it
A request that needs more is checked in this order, and the first check that fails answers:
403 account-required: the key belongs to no account.
403 plan-ended or 403 key-withheld: the key is withheld; the message says why.
403 plan-required: the plan does not open the paid market. The body names the cheapest tier on sale in required_plan. An included market the plan does not open answers 403 market-not-in-plan.
404 level-not-served when the market does not serve the level, with its levels; 403 level-required when the tier does not sell it, naming the cheapest tier that does.
403 history-limit: the dates start before the tier's history for that level, counted back from today at 00:00 UTC. The body gives plan, level, earliest, requested_from and the next tier that reaches further. The included markets have no window.
GET /v3/preview?market=cme&symbol=ESZ6&timeframe=1m          (a free key)
403  {"code": "plan-required", "message": "This feed requires a subscription",
      "required_plan": {"market": "cme", "tier": "lite", "name": "CME Group Lite", "subscribe_url": "/data?tab=subscriptions"}}
GET /v3/export?market=cme&symbol=ESZ6&timeframe=tick&data=book&from=2026-11-05 14:30:00&to=2026-11-05 14:31:00&format=csv          (a CME Group Lite key)
403  {"code": "level-required", "level": "L2", "message": "Level 2 book needs CME Group Standard or above",
      "required_plan": {"market": "cme", "tier": "standard", "name": "CME Group Standard", "subscribe_url": "/data?tab=subscriptions"}}

symbols
Symbols
A symbol is spelt as /v3/pairs lists it, and matched in any case: aapl finds AAPL. Every instrument also has a number, pair_id, which may be given instead of the symbol. Nasdaq TotalView uses the numbers of us_stocks: NVDA is 2000008377 in both.
Market | Spelling | Example
US stocks, Nasdaq TotalView | The ticker | AAPL
UK stocks, Canadian stocks | The ticker, without a suffix | VOD, RY
Forex | The pair with an underscore; its name has a slash | EUR_USD
Crypto | Quoted in Tether | BTCUSDT
Futures | The contract family | CRUDEOIL, SP500
Indices, commodities | As listed | SPX, XAU/USD
CME Globex
The contract's exchange name; #cme-market | The market: cme has the rules
ESZ6, NGZ26
US options | The OCC symbol exactly as listed: capitals, the root padded to six characters | AAPL  261120C00100000
A search with q looks in symbols and names, so q=EUR/USD finds EUR_USD, while symbol=EUR/USD finds nothing. An option contract written without its padding, or in lower case, answers 404 pair-unknown, and the sentence shows the padded form to write; the Python client adds the padding for you.
A US stock symbol that was renamed still answers a request whose first day is on or before the last day the exchange listed it: the rows are the instrument's, the file is named by the symbol it has now, an export says so in x-lse3-symbol and an estimate or a preview in renamed. Without dates, or for later dates, it answers 404 symbol-unknown and the sentence names the new symbol. A pair_id never changes with a rename.
dates
Dates and times
Form | Example | Selects
Two dates | from=2026-10-08&to=2026-10-14 | Exchange trading days, both ends included
Two UTC timestamps | from=2026-10-08 13:30:00&to=2026-10-08 13:35:00 | From the first instant up to the second, which is excluded. A fraction, a T between date and time and a trailing Z are accepted.
Refusal | When
400 date-mixed | A date on one side and a timestamp on the other.
400 time-bad | A timestamp without seconds.
400 date-bad | A date that does not exist.
400 date-order | `from` after `to`.
400 date-missing | An export without `from` and `to`. Previews and estimates may leave both out.
404 date-not-trading | CME Globex or futures dates with no session.
Daily, weekly and monthly candles follow exchange sessions. A daily candle's time is its first minute with a trade, in UTC: AAPL's candle for 1 October 2026 reads 2026-10-01 04:02:00.
answers
Answers and refusals
A JSON answer is an object. Lists are paged with limit and offset, and a page carries total, count, limit and offset; an offset past the end is an empty page. A refusal is an object with code and message, plus the fields named for that code, and its HTTP status says the kind.
Status | Kind
400 | The request is wrong.
401 | The key.
403 | The plan.
404 | Nothing there.
410 | Gone.
413 | Too wide.
416 | A range past the end of a file.
429 | Too many, the weekly allowance, or the rows a day.
502 | The data store refused the query.
503 | Busy or unavailable; try again.
504 | Timed out.
Every code is listed under #refusals | Refusal codes. Browsers may call the server only from londonstrategicedge.com, so call it from your own code, not from a web page on another site.
files
Files
An export answers the bytes at once: 200 with Content-Disposition naming the file, Content-Length, x-checksum-sha256 (the file's SHA-256), x-export-id (the server's name for this export in its log), x-export-rows (the rows it charged) and x-lse3-adjusted 1 or 0. The file is built whole in the server's memory before the first byte goes out, so the size and the checksum are exact, and nothing is kept once it has been sent.
A download that is cut is requested again from the start. prepare=1 is accepted and ignored. /v3/jobs/<id> and /v3/jobs/<id>/file, the prepared-file routes of earlier servers, answer 404 export-unknown.
one-download
One download at a time
A key has one download open at a time, on every plan. While a file of the key is streaming, one more export, dataset export, macro or reference export answers 429 connections-exhausted, naming the file to wait for. While a job of the key is building, one more export answers 429 exports-busy with the same message; both carry Retry-After: 5. Every build takes a place in one of the server's two lanes, a fast one for small builds and a long one for the rest, each served in arrival order; a build that finds its lane full waits in a queue, its job building in the phase queued, and the export call answers 202 past its 10 seconds as usual. Only when the queue is full, or no counter was free to measure the export, does the export answer 503 exports-busy with Retry-After: 30; send it again then.
GET /v3/export?market=us_stocks&symbol=AAPL&timeframe=1m&from=2021-10-21&to=2026-10-22&format=parquet      (sent while the same key's first file was still downloading)
429  {"code": "connections-exhausted", "message": "One download at a time. AAPL_us_stocks_1m_2021-10-21_2026-10-21.parquet is still coming."}
Downloads are paced per key: 50 Mbit/s on the free plan and 250 Mbit/s on a paid plan, after a first 4 MiB at full speed. The free plan also has a weekly allowance of 12.5 GiB, counted on exports and job files. Past it every export answers 429 allowance-exhausted until Monday 00:00 UTC.
429  {"code": "allowance-exhausted", "message": "Your plan's weekly allowance of 12.5 GiB is used up (12.63 GiB this week); it resets Monday 00:00 UTC. A paid plan has no weekly allowance."}
rows
Rows per export and per day
Every export is measured in rows before it is built. Trades, quotes, orders and the book of one instrument are counted from the stored rows, the count stopping at the plan's rows an export plus one: the rows charged are the rows of the file. Candles are measured by the stored bars the file is made from: a width that is stored is its own rows; a day, a week or a month read from the stored daily candles is one row a session; a file folded from finer bars is measured by the bars it reads, which /v3/estimate names as measured_rows (daily candles of a market without stored days, forex among them, read its minute bars). The plan sets two figures, which GET /v3/usage reports under rows.
Plan | Rows one export may read | Rows a day
Free | 2,500,000 | 12,500,000, reset at 00:00 UTC
Paid tiers | 2,500,000 | No daily limit
Past the rows an export the request answers 413 export-too-wide before anything is built, with both figures in the sentence. Past the rows a day it answers 429 rows-exhausted, with Retry-After and retry_after giving the seconds to 00:00 UTC. The rows are charged once, when the build is admitted, so used_today moves before the file exists; nothing is charged for a selection with no rows, a refused export or a resumed file, and a build that fails on our side gives its rows back. An export the server could not measure within 10 seconds is not built: it answers 413 export-too-wide saying so; choose shorter dates.
GET /v3/export?market=us_stocks&symbol=AAPL&timeframe=1m&from=2003-01-01&to=2026-10-24&format=csv      (a free key)
413  {"code": "export-too-wide", "message": "This export would read 4,132,385 rows; your plan allows at most 2,500,000 rows an export. Choose shorter dates."}
GET /v3/export?market=us_stocks&symbol=AAPL&timeframe=1d&from=2000-01-01&to=2026-10-24&format=csv      (a free key with 11,900,000 rows used today)
429  {"code": "rows-exhausted", "message": "Your plan allows 12,500,000 rows a day; 11,900,000 are used and this export would read 6,743. It resets at 00:00 UTC, in 5 h 12 min.", "retry_after": 18720}

pairs
/v3/pairs
A market's instruments, searched and paged. Without a market it searches every enabled market. us_options pages by cursor; cme and us_options can be grouped by underlying.
Parameters
Parameter | Meaning
market | The folder; leave it out to search every market
symbol | Exactly this symbol, any case
q | A search in symbols and names (on `us_options` at most 128 characters). The rows come by rank: the symbol is `q`, begins with it, holds it, then the name holds it; then the symbols that hold `q` once `/`, `-`, `_`, `.` and spaces are dropped on both sides (`BTC/USD` finds BTCUSDT, `BRK-B` finds BRK.B), and on `cme` the contracts of a product family whose name holds a `q` of three characters or more (`gold` finds GC and MGC)
limit, offset | Rows a page, 0 to 10000 (100 when not given), and rows to skip
sort
symbol (default) or popular: a short list of familiar instruments first, then the rest by symbol
cursor
On us_options: the next_cursor of the page before, sent back unchanged with the same filters
group_by
underlying, on cme or us_options: lists underlyings instead of contracts
underlying
On cme or us_options: the contracts of one underlying, by the id the grouped list gives, such as SP500 or AAPL
product, expiry, instrument_type, option_type
With underlying: one product; one expiry day; future, option, spread, strategy, other or all; call, put or all
Answer
Field | Meaning
market, total, count, limit, offset | The page
sort, sort_basis
The order asked for, and the one used: symbol, editorial or search_relevance
pairs[] | `folder`, `symbol`, `name`, `pair_id`, `company_id` and `photo` where known, `active`, `listed_date`, `delisted_date`, `splits[]`, `availability`, and the stored tick count
tick_count | The instrument's stored rows (stored trades for an option contract), with `tick_count_basis`, `tick_count_status` (`counted`, `not-collected`, `unavailable`) and `tick_count_as_of`
tick_first, tick_last | The first and last day of the count, or `null`
next_cursor, has_more | On `us_options`: the next page. A first page and a page by `offset` count the search for `total`; a page by `next_cursor` carries the count its search took within the last minute, so the pages of one walk agree and a cursor page's `total` can be up to a minute old
underlyings[] | With `group_by`: `id`, `symbol`, `name`, `products`, `contract_count`, `active_contract_count`

market-data
Market data
Five calls read prices: preview, estimate, export, its second name candles, and slice. They take the same selection.
selection
The selection
Parameter | Meaning
market | The folder; required
symbol or pair_id | The instrument; required unless `underlying` is given
underlying | US options only: every contract of one root (`META`, `SPXW`) in one file, in place of a symbol. The contracts are those listed for the dates (not expired before `from`, listed by `to`), at most 20,000 (`413 contracts-too-many` past that: choose one expiry or shorter dates). Every row begins with `symbol` and `pair_id`; the file is sorted by symbol, then time; prices are as published. The file is `<ROOT>_contracts[_<expiry>][_calls|_puts]_us_options_<timeframe>_<from>_<to>`
expiry, option_type | With `underlying`: one expiry day (`YYYY-MM-DD`); `call`, `put` or `all` (the default)
data | `candles` (default), `trades`, `quotes`, `orders` (Level 3), `book` (Level 2), `greeks` (US options)
timeframe | `tick`, `1s`, `5s`, `15s`, `30s`, `1m` (default), `3m`, `5m`, `15m`, `30m`, `1h`, `4h`, `1d`, `1w`, `1mo`. `tick` with `candles` is the trade slice. Greeks need `1m` or longer.
from, to | Two dates or two UTC timestamps; required for an export
format | `parquet` (default) or `csv`
adjusted | `1` (default) adjusts prices and volumes for splits; `0` gives them as published, as does `as_published=1`
What each data returns
data | Columns | Where
candles | time, open, high, low, close, volume, trades | Every market
tick | time, price, size, kind | Every market with trades; not forex or commodities
trades | time, price, size, kind | CME Globex, Nasdaq TotalView
quotes | time, bid_price, ask_price, bid_size, ask_size | CME Globex, Nasdaq TotalView
orders | time, action, side, order_id, price, size | Level 3: CME Globex, Nasdaq TotalView
book
time, sequence, action, side, price, size, depth, then ten bid and ten ask levels of price, size and orders: 67 columns
Level 2: CME Globex, Nasdaq TotalView
greeks | time, price_at, iv, delta, gamma, theta, vega, option_price, price_source, underlying_price, years_to_expiry, rate, dividends_pv | US options
Trades, quotes, orders and book on an included market answer 400 data-unavailable ("This market offers timeframes, including tick"). Forex and commodities store quotes, not trades, so tick on them answers 404 data-unavailable. Sub-minute candles are built from 1-second bars, 1m to 4h from 1-minute bars. A Greek the formula cannot give is null, \N in CSV.

estimate
/v3/estimate
The rows and bytes an export would have, and what the export will be held to. Trades, quotes, orders and the book of one instrument are counted from the stored rows, the count stopping at the plan's rows an export plus one, so the figure is the one the export is charged; candles are read from the stored bar counts. Without from and to it describes the newest-ten-rows window of the preview.
Answer
Field | Meaning
rows | The rows of the file. Ticks: counted from the stored rows; for a selection past the plan's rows an export, the month figures or a wider count, and `null` with a `basis` that begins "not counted yet" when neither answers. Candles: the stored bar counts of the months the dates touch, a month at either end in proportion
measured_rows | Present when the export is held to more rows than its file has: the stored bars a folded candle file reads
estimated_bytes | The size of the file to expect
ceiling_bytes, mode | The most the file can weigh, and `stream` or `file`: above 100 MB a browser should write the file to disk as it arrives
filename | The name the file will have
basis | What the figure was read from
download | The refusal a download would meet: for a selection the key may not download, with `required_plan`; for one past the plan's rows, the export's own `413 export-too-wide` or `429 rows-exhausted`
renamed | Present when the symbol asked is one the instrument carried before a rename: `asked`, the `symbol` it has now, its `pair_id`
message | Why the rows are 0, when they are

Options
Both calls read us_options, one of the included markets, and need a key that belongs to an account.
us-options
The market: us_options
67.6 million US option contracts listed since 1 April 2013, on 14,138 underlyings: stocks, ETFs and indices. Candles start on 1 April 2013. Trades start on 18 January 2024, each as the exchanges report it on the OPRA feed, with the venue letter. On 14 November 2026 the market held 14.9 billion candles of 1 second, 1 minute and 15 minutes, 579 million daily candles and 5.46 billion trades. It answers:
Contracts: /v3/pairs?market=us_options, paged by cursor, by underlying, or grouped by underlying.
Candles at every timeframe, from 1 April 2013.
The trade slice, timeframe=tick, from 18 January 2024: time, price, size, kind, for example 2026-10-21 13:34:17.213383276 | 239.25 | 6 | trade. data=trades belongs to the paid feeds and answers 400 data-unavailable here.
Greeks, data=greeks, from 1 July 2024: one row per traded minute, computed twice a day.
Splits: /v3/factors on a contract answers the underlying's splits.
There are no quotes, book or orders: the feed carries trades. Before 18 January 2024 there are no ticks, and before 1 July 2024 no Greeks. The session of 18 June 2021 is missing. A contract that did not trade in the dates asked answers rows 0 with a message from the estimate, and 404 data-unavailable from the export.

options-flow
/v3/options/flow
Trades of the OPRA feed with their premium (price × size × 100), newest first, each with its venue, dte (days from the trade's day to the expiry, 0 on the expiry day) and the Greeks of its minute where that day's figures are computed. With underlying the trades are those of every contract that could have traded in the window, its expiry on or after the window's first day, whether or not it has expired since, and the chain's filters apply, min_dte and max_dte judged on each day of the window. A series carries symbol, name, country, rows, first, last, and company_id with photo where it names a company.

reference-series
/v3/reference/preview · estimate · export
dataset is required. symbol selects one series; leave it out for the whole collection. country and q filter, and from and to bound the time column, both included; leaving them out selects the whole archive, rows without a date included. The preview answers dataset, symbol, columns and the last ten rows; the estimate rows, estimated_bytes, mode and filename; the export every native column as csv or parquet, with prepare.

paid-feeds
Paid feeds: what changes with a plan
A key of a CME Group or Nasdaq tier opens its market's candles, trades and quotes from Lite, the Level 2 book and Level 3 orders as the tier sells them, within the tier's months of history per level. A paid plan has no weekly allowance, downloads at 250 Mbit/s, and has no daily rows limit; one export still reads at most 2,500,000 rows. Without the tier, the market's rows answer 403 plan-required; its figures answer every key. CME Globex and Nasdaq TotalView serve L1, L2 and L3. The /pricing | pricing page shows each tier's limits and a sample of each feed.

cme-market
The market: cme
The contracts of CME Globex: futures, options, spreads and strategies, with candles, trades, quotes, the Level 2 book and the Level 3 orders. The same contracts stream live on the /websocket-documentation#cme | CME Group stream, under the same names.
Names: a contract goes by its exchange name: the product, the month letter and the last digit of the year, as in ESZ6. Natural gas carries two digits of the year, as in NGZ26, and so does crude oil from 2030, as in CLZ30, so read a name from the catalogue instead of building it. /v3/pairs?market=cme&group_by=underlying lists the products, and underlying=SP500&instrument_type=future one product's contracts.
A name comes round again after ten years: ESZ6 is the contract of December 2026 and the one of December 2016. With dates, a symbol means the contract that was listed on the first day you ask for, by the listed_date and delisted_date of the listing below, so ESZ6 from a day of 2016 is the contract of 2016. Without dates, or when no contract of that name was listed on that day, it means the contract that trades now, or else the one listed last. pair_id always names one contract.
Dates are trade dates: a trade date opens the evening before, at 23:00 UTC in winter and 22:00 UTC in summer, so from=2026-11-05&to=2026-11-05 runs from 2026-11-04 23:00:00 up to 2026-11-05 22:00:00 UTC. A date with no session, such as a Saturday, answers 404 date-not-trading.
The newest date: the trades, quotes and candles of an exchange day are loaded the morning after it, UTC, and its book and orders after them. Until then its trade date answers only what is already stored, the part of its evening before 00:00 UTC, and a later date answers 404 data-unavailable.
Sizes: for the trade date 5 November 2026, ESZ6 has 1,380 one-minute candles and 1,257,319 trades, 62 MB as CSV. Its book for the one minute from 14:30 UTC is 99,104 rows and 33 MB, and for the whole date 10.8 GB. Ask for the book and the orders by time, with two UTC timestamps, an hour or less of a busy contract at a time.

import os

cme-options-market
The market: cme_options
The options on CME Globex's futures, served as candles. A key that opens cme opens cme_options, under the same plan and the same months of history. On 14 November 2026 it listed 16.5 million contracts on 3,962 underlying products, each linked to the future it is on.
Contracts: /v3/pairs?market=cme_options with one of symbol, underlying (a future, as in ESZ6) or product (the option product, as in E1C), narrowed by expiry, option_type and active. A contract goes by its exchange name, as in E1CZ6 P100, or by its pair_id.
Candles of 1 minute and wider. Daily, weekly and monthly candles follow CME Globex's trade dates, as they do for cme.
Not served yet: trades, quotes, the book, orders, Greeks and timeframes under a minute answer 400 data-unavailable.
The history is being loaded from the newest day backwards, to 6 June 2010. On 14 November 2026 it had reached 25 July 2021: 123 million one-minute candles on 2.9 million contracts that traded.

The Python client
Install the lse-data package, then client = LSE(api_key). The same package streams live prices; see the /websocket-documentation | WebSocket documentation. The source is on https://github.com/londonstrategicedge/lse-data | _blank | noopener noreferrer | GitHub.
python -m pip install lse-data
import os

markets = client.markets()

)
macro = client.macro_catalogue()
reference = client.reference_catalogue()
client.dataset_export(
    "us_stocks", "AAPL", "5m", "2026-10-08", "2026-10-14",

The client keeps the key in the header and follows no redirect. It accepts a job's download_url only on the server's own origin, resumes an interrupted file with Range and If-Range from its .part file, and checks the size and the SHA-256 before it places the file. A refusal raises LSEError with status, code, message and required_plan. An export that outlasts its timeout raises it with code timeout; the same call again continues it. A call past the plan's calls a minute waits once for Retry-After and tries again, unless the client was made with wait_for_limit=False.
retired
Retired endpoints
New integrations use LSE Data 3.0. The earlier /v1, /databank-api and /iso routes on api.londonstrategicedge.com are retired and answer 410 Gone with a Link header that points here.
The published LSE Terminal keeps its existing data calls: /vault/catalog, /vault/candles, /vault/usage, /vault/meta, /vault/reference, /vault/options/chain, /vault/options/flow, /vault/ref/economic_calendar and /vault/series. These require an API key and use the data available for the selected instrument. Other /vault routes, including exports, tick history and other reference datasets, remain retired. The terminal's prices and screener remain available at data-api.londonstrategicedge.com/v1/prices and /v1/screener.
