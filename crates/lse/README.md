# LSE

A NautilusTrader adapter for London Strategic Edge's market data (LSE Data 3.0), laid out as Nautilus' adapters are: a data client only, as LSE trades nothing, naming no symbol. It serves the trades of LSE's derived markets, their tick slices, as `TradeTick`s, and the trades and quotes of CME Globex contracts, Level 1, as `TradeTick`s and `QuoteTick`s; every other request fails as Nautilus' data client default does (unsupported), and every subscription is logged as not implemented. A derived market's quotes are asked of LSE, which refuses them (`400 data-unavailable`: it serves a derived market's past as timeframes alone). It decides nothing a key may have: it asks LSE, and LSE's refusal is the request's failure.

## Instruments

A series of a derived market (futures, stocks, indices, forex, commodities, crypto) is one instrument under the venue `LSEDGE`, not `LSE`, which is IB's London Stock Exchange. Its symbol is LSE's symbol, a hyphen and LSE's market in capitals: GOLD in `futures` is `GOLD-FUTURES.LSEDGE` (`LseSymbol`). LSE lists one symbol in several markets, and its market names have no hyphen, so the last hyphen splits symbol from market. An exchange market's listings (`cme`, `nasdaq`, `us_options`) are not `LSEDGE` series.

A CME Globex futures contract is the instrument Databento gives it, under `GLBX` (LSE's `XCME`), in Nautilus' two-digit year (`FuturesYearDigits::Two`): `ESZ26.GLBX` is the E-mini S&P 500 of December 2026. CME names a contract by its product, its month letter and its year's last digit, two for natural gas and, from 2030, crude oil, so a name comes round every ten years: LSE's `ESZ6` names the contracts of 2016 and of 2026. So a contract is asked of LSE by the number of its listing (`pair_id`), which names it alone, looked up once a client in LSE's catalogue (`/v3/pairs?market=cme&symbol=…`) under its year's last digit, then its last two (`ESZ6`, then `ESZ26`): the listing whose name says the contract's month (`ES futures 2026-12`) or, for one LSE names by its symbol alone (some delisted contracts, as `GCZ4` of 2024), the one delisted from three months before the contract's month to its end. A one-digit year, a root, a spread or an option is refused naming it. Nautilus' futures symbol functions (`identifiers::futures`) parse and write the names.

LSE publishes no instrument definitions. The client answers an instrument request with none, a completed envelope with no payload, and converts LSE's prices and sizes at the precisions of the instrument in its cache, so the definition must reach the cache from elsewhere, such as a catalog Nautilus answers the instrument request from first or, for a CME contract, Databento's data client. A request of an instrument the cache lacks fails naming it, before LSE is asked.

## Credentials

LSE issues a key for its own markets and, with an exchange plan, a key of the exchange's, which authenticates only where its market is carried (`/v3/usage`, `product`). The client takes each, given in its config or read when the node creates the client: the LSE key, `api_key` or `LSE_API_KEY`, for the derived markets, and a CME Group plan's key, `cme_api_key` or `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, for CME Globex (`LseProduct`). Either may be missing: a request of a market whose key is missing fails naming its variable, before LSE is asked, and a client with neither is not created. Each key goes in the `X-API-Key` header of its market's calls; no `Debug` output and no error holds it, and redirects are refused, so that it goes nowhere else.

## Exports

A trades request of a derived market's series asks LSE's tick slice (`/v3/export`, `timeframe=tick`); of a CME contract, its trades (`data=trades`), and a quotes request its quotes (`data=quotes`), each by its listing's number (`pair_id`). Each is asked as Parquet, at prices as published (`adjusted=0`), a UTC day an export over two timestamps, `[from, to)`. An export LSE refuses as too wide (`413 export-too-wide`: past the 2,500,000 rows one export may read on every plan, or not counted within LSE's 10 seconds) is asked again in halves, and a half LSE still refuses is halved again, down to a second, past which LSE's refusal fails the request: no second of any series LSE serves holds that many rows. LSE answers an export with its file: since 6 November 2026 it keeps no prepared file (`/v3/jobs` answers `export-unknown`), so there is no job to poll. A download cut short is fetched again, as LSE's docs say. A whole file whose SHA-256 is not the one LSE gives with it (`x-checksum-sha256`) fails the request, naming the export by its selection and by LSE's id of it (`x-export-id`), for LSE to look into: LSE builds a file whole before sending it, so that its checksum is exact. One export downloads at a time, as LSE allows one a key.

Each key's calls are spaced to stay within the calls a minute of its plan in any minute, each key counting its own: the config's `calls_per_minute` for the LSE key, by default the free plan's 60, the fewest of any plan, and `cme_calls_per_minute` for the CME key, by default CME Group Lite's 200, the fewest of its plans; set the plan's figure (`/v3/usage`, `calls_per_minute`) to go faster. Each key downloads one export at a time. A catalogue lookup is asked again for a refusal for the moment as an export is. A refusal for the moment, a 429 of calls or of the one download (`calls-exhausted`, `connections-exhausted`, `exports-busy`) or a busy 503, is asked again after the `Retry-After` LSE gives, or a backoff, ten times at most. A 429 of a quota (`allowance-exhausted`, `rows-exhausted`) fails the request at once with LSE's message: it lasts until Monday or until 00:00 UTC, and what the request did not get is the caller's to ask for again once it resets. A selection LSE holds no row of (`404 data-unavailable`, `date-not-trading`) answers no rows; a `400 data-unavailable` says the market serves no such rows, and fails the request. Every other refusal fails the request with LSE's status, code, message and required plan, for example `403 plan-required: This feed requires a subscription; required plan: CME Group Lite`.

LSE stores a UTC day after it ends, the morning after it (its book and orders later still), and answers a day it has not loaded as one it holds no row of (`data-unavailable`: "the dates asked may not be loaded yet (or nothing traded in them)"). So the client takes the current UTC day as not loaded, whatever LSE answers, and the day before as not loaded until LSE answers rows of it. A request reaching into that span is answered with what LSE holds in a plain `DataResponse` rather than a completed `DataResponseEnvelope`, and the span is logged: the request completes, but Nautilus' data engine writes a payload-only reply to no catalog, as it certifies no coverage, so that a fill asks for the window again on its next run rather than recording a day LSE had not loaded as one with no trades. Nautilus' OKX client answers a window it cannot confirm the same way.

LSE sends prices and sizes as doubles. Each is converted once, through its shortest decimal form, which must fit the instrument's precision: a finer price fails the request rather than being rounded, as a definition too coarse would round it. A trade is stamped at LSE's time, both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, with no aggressor side, LSE sending none; its trade id is a hash (FNV-1a, as Nautilus' Databento adapter derives ids) of its instrument, time, price, size and its place among the trades alike before it in the file, so trades alike keep ids apart and a refill gives the same ids. A quote is stamped at LSE's time too, with the bid and ask sizes LSE sends; a quote with an empty side, a price LSE's schema allows to be empty, gives no `QuoteTick`, as Nautilus' Databento decoder gives none of a record with an undefined side (`decode_mbp1_msg`).

## Tests

The tests run against a mock LSE peer (`testing`) replaying payloads captured from LSE with the free key and the CME Group Lite+ key, and LSE's documented answers where none was captured (`test_data/`, see its README). With the `test-support` feature the peer and its payloads serve a caller's tests of its own fill through the adapter. They run with the workspace's, or alone:

```sh
cargo test -p lse
```

A test against LSE itself needs a key, so its caller holds and gates it, as this repository's caller does (`NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1`, see the root README).
