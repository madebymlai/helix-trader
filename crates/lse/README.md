# LSE

A NautilusTrader adapter for London Strategic Edge's market data (LSE Data 3.0), laid out as Nautilus' adapters are: a data client only, as LSE trades nothing, naming no symbol. It serves the trades of LSE's derived markets, their tick slices, as `TradeTick`s, LSE's candles, 1 second to 4 hours wide, as external `Bar`s stamped at their close, the trades and quotes of CME Globex contracts, Level 1, as `TradeTick`s and `QuoteTick`s, and the rows of LSE's reference collections as custom data (`LseRow`, see [reference rows](#reference-rows)); every other request fails as Nautilus' data client default does (unsupported), and every subscription is logged as not implemented. A derived market's quotes are asked of LSE, which refuses them (`400 data-unavailable`: it serves a derived market's past as timeframes alone). It decides nothing a key may have: it asks LSE, and LSE's refusal is the request's failure.

## Instruments

A series of a derived market (futures, stocks, indices, forex, commodities, crypto) is one instrument under the venue `LSEDGE`, not `LSE`, which is IB's London Stock Exchange. Its symbol is LSE's symbol, a hyphen and LSE's market in capitals: GOLD in `futures` is `GOLD-FUTURES.LSEDGE` (`LseSymbol`). LSE lists one symbol in several markets, and its market names have no hyphen, so the last hyphen splits symbol from market. An exchange market's listings (`cme`, `nasdaq`, `us_options`) are not `LSEDGE` series.

A CME Globex futures contract is the instrument Databento gives it, under `GLBX` (LSE's `XCME`), in Nautilus' two-digit year (`FuturesYearDigits::Two`): `ESZ26.GLBX` is the E-mini S&P 500 of December 2026. CME names a contract by its product, its month letter and its year's last digit, two for natural gas and, from 2030, crude oil, so a name comes round every ten years: LSE's `ESZ6` names the contracts of 2016 and of 2026. So a contract is asked of LSE by the number of its listing (`pair_id`), which names it alone, looked up once a client in LSE's catalogue (`/v3/pairs?market=cme&symbol=…`) under its year's last digit, then its last two (`ESZ6`, then `ESZ26`): the listing whose name says the contract's month (`ES futures 2026-12`) or, for one LSE names by its symbol alone (some delisted contracts, as `GCZ4` of 2024), the one delisted from three months before the contract's month to its end. A one-digit year, a root, a spread or an option is refused naming it. Nautilus' futures symbol functions (`identifiers::futures`) parse and write the names.

LSE publishes no instrument definitions. The client fails an instrument request naming the instrument, so a definition the catalog lacks fails where it is asked for (Nautilus' data engine asks the client only for one its catalogs lack), and converts LSE's prices and sizes at the precisions of the instrument in its cache, so the definition must reach the cache from elsewhere, such as a catalog Nautilus answers the instrument request from first or, for a CME contract, Databento's data client. A request of an instrument the cache lacks fails naming it, before LSE is asked.

## Credentials

LSE issues a key for its own markets and, with an exchange plan, a key of the exchange's, which authenticates only where its market is carried (`/v3/usage`, `product`). The client takes each, given in its config or read when the node creates the client: the LSE key, `api_key` or `LSE_API_KEY`, for the derived markets, and a CME Group plan's key, `cme_api_key` or `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, for CME Globex (`LseProduct`). Either may be missing: a request of a market whose key is missing fails naming its variable, before LSE is asked, and a client with neither is not created. Each key goes in the `X-API-Key` header of its market's calls; no `Debug` output and no error holds it, and redirects are refused, so that it goes nowhere else.

## Exports

A trades request of a derived market's series asks LSE's tick slice (`/v3/export`, `timeframe=tick`); of a CME contract, its trades (`data=trades`), and a quotes request its quotes (`data=quotes`), each by its listing's number (`pair_id`). Each is asked as Parquet, at prices as published (`adjusted=0`), a UTC day an export over two timestamps, `[from, to)`. A request's window is closed, `[start, end]`, so it is asked as `[start, end + 1 ns)`, as Nautilus' Databento client asks one; a window ending at a midnight, as Nautilus' day windows from a midnight do, has that midnight's instant asked in its last day's export, LSE serving exports across midnight, rather than in an export of that instant alone. An export LSE refuses as too wide (`413 export-too-wide`: past the 2,500,000 rows one export may read on every plan, or not counted within LSE's 10 seconds) is asked again in halves, and a half LSE still refuses is halved again, down to a second, past which LSE's refusal fails the request: no second of any series LSE serves holds that many rows. LSE answers an export with its file: since 6 November 2026 it keeps no prepared file (`/v3/jobs` answers `export-unknown`), so there is no job to poll. A download cut short is fetched again, as LSE's docs say. LSE's file is taken only from a 200 carrying its SHA-256 (`x-checksum-sha256`), as LSE sends every file; any other success fails the request as an answer LSE's docs do not describe, with its status. A whole file whose SHA-256 is not the one LSE gives with it fails the request, naming the export by its selection and by LSE's id of it (`x-export-id`), for LSE to look into: LSE builds a file whole before sending it, so that its checksum is exact. One export downloads at a time, as LSE allows one a key.

Each key's calls are spaced to stay within the calls a minute of its plan in any minute, each key counting its own: the config's `calls_per_minute` for the LSE key, by default the free plan's 60, the fewest of any plan, and `cme_calls_per_minute` for the CME key, by default CME Group Lite's 200, the fewest of its plans; set the plan's figure (`/v3/usage`, `calls_per_minute`) to go faster. Each key downloads one export at a time. A catalogue lookup is asked again for a refusal for the moment as an export is. A refusal for the moment, a 429 of calls or of the one download (`calls-exhausted`, `connections-exhausted`, `exports-busy`) or a busy 503, is asked again after the `Retry-After` LSE gives, or a backoff, ten times at most. A 429 of a quota (`allowance-exhausted`, `rows-exhausted`) fails the request at once with LSE's message: it lasts until Monday or until 00:00 UTC, and what the request did not get is the caller's to ask for again once it resets. A selection LSE holds no row of answers no rows: `date-not-trading`, and a `data-unavailable` whose message begins "No stored data for this selection", which LSE's docs give as the only one for a selection with no stored row; any other `data-unavailable`, such as a tick slice of a market storing no trades (forex, commodities) or a derived market's quotes, fails the request as other refusals do. Every other refusal fails the request with LSE's status, code, message and required plan, for example `403 plan-required: This feed requires a subscription; required plan: CME Group Lite`.

LSE stores a UTC day after it ends, the morning after it (its book and orders later still), and answers a day it has not loaded as one it holds no row of (`data-unavailable`: "the dates asked may not be loaded yet (or nothing traded in them)"). So the client takes the current UTC day as not loaded, whatever LSE answers, and the day before as not loaded until LSE answers rows of it. A window ending at a midnight reaches into the next day by that instant, and so into that span if the next day is in it. A request reaching into that span is answered with what LSE holds in a plain `DataResponse` rather than a completed `DataResponseEnvelope`, and the span is logged: the request completes, but Nautilus' data engine writes a payload-only reply to no catalog, as it certifies no coverage, so that a fill asks for the window again on its next run rather than recording a day LSE had not loaded as one with no rows. Nautilus' OKX client answers a window it cannot confirm the same way.

A bars request asks LSE's candles (`/v3/export`, `data=candles`, LSE's default) of the bar type's width, as published and as Parquet, as trades are. LSE serves candles 1, 5, 15 and 30 seconds, 1, 3, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, and 1 and 4 hours wide, as `1-SECOND-LAST-EXTERNAL` to `4-HOUR-LAST-EXTERNAL` bars (`CandleWidth`), each of LSE's widths one bar specification: `60-MINUTE` is not `1-HOUR`. A candle has one price, the last: of trades where its market has trades, and of LSE's stored quotes in forex and commodities, which store no trades (LSE's docs; gold spot's candles have no volume). Any other bar type is refused naming it, before LSE is asked: a width LSE does not serve, another price, bars Nautilus builds (`INTERNAL`), and LSE's daily, weekly and monthly candles, which follow exchange sessions (each stamped at its session's first traded minute and folded from the sessions inside the dates asked), so that no close of theirs is known. LSE stamps a candle at its open, on a grid of its width from 1970 in UTC; its bar is stamped at its close, its open plus its width (Nautilus' `BarSpecification::timedelta`), both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, as Nautilus' adapters stamp bars by default (Databento's `bars_timestamp_on_close`). A request over `[start, end]` gets the bars closing in it. LSE folds a candle from the rows inside the export alone, so a candle cut by the export's start comes partial: the client asks only whole candles, from the first one's open to the last one's close, a UTC day an export as trades are, and nothing if no candle closes in the window; an export of candles LSE refuses as too wide is halved between the two candles nearest its middle, and one of a single candle, which no width holds too many rows for, is not.

LSE sends prices and sizes as doubles. Each is converted once, through its shortest decimal form, which must fit the instrument's precision: a finer price fails the request rather than being rounded, as a definition too coarse would round it; a candle's volume is a size. A trade is stamped at LSE's time, both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, with no aggressor side, LSE sending none; its trade id is a hash (FNV-1a, as Nautilus' Databento adapter derives ids) of its instrument, time, price, size and its place among the trades alike before it in the file, so trades alike keep ids apart and a refill gives the same ids. A quote is stamped at LSE's time too, with the bid and ask sizes LSE sends; a quote with an empty side, a price LSE's schema allows to be empty, gives no `QuoteTick`, as Nautilus' Databento decoder gives none of a record with an undefined side (`decode_mbp1_msg`).

## Reference rows

Every LSE row that is not a price is one custom data type, `LseRow`, made with Nautilus' custom data macros (`docs/concepts/custom_data.md`): its source, the collection it is of (`bond_yields`), its symbol (`US10Y`), its time, and the row's other fields as LSE sent them, in JSON (numbers, strings, `null`, dates and times in ISO 8601). It is asked through Nautilus' custom data request, `request_data`, of a data type naming the source and the symbol in its metadata: `LseRow::data_type("bond_yields", "US10Y")`, which keys the series in a catalog by the identifier `US10Y-BOND_YIELDS`, LSE's symbol, a hyphen and its source in capitals, as an `LSEDGE` series' symbol names its market. The client answers with the request's data type, so the data engine writes the rows back under that identifier, and registers `LseRow` with Nautilus when it is created; a reader of stored rows registers it first (`LseRow::register`), as Nautilus decodes custom data by its type's name. Of LSE's calls it serves the reference collections (`/v3/reference`): a source that is no collection of LSE's catalogue fails naming it, and a data type that is not `LseRow`'s, or names no source or symbol, fails before LSE is asked.

A request over `[start, end]` asks LSE's catalogue (`/v3/reference`) for the column of the collection's time (`time_column`), then the collection's export of the symbol (`/v3/reference/export`) over the UTC dates the window spans, both included, as Parquet: LSE refuses a time (`filter-bad`) for a collection keyed by dates and selects a day's rows by its date either way. Each row is stamped at its time, both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, a date at its start in UTC, and the rows outside `[start, end]` are left out. A row of a date is thus stamped at the start of its day, as LSE names it, though a daily close is known only at the day's end: a reader allows for that. The export is downloaded as a market's is (see [exports](#exports)): one at a time, checked by its SHA-256, a refusal for the moment asked again, and a selection LSE holds no row of (`data-unavailable`, "No stored data for this selection") answers no rows.

## Tests

The tests run against a mock LSE peer (`testing`) replaying payloads captured from LSE with the free key and the CME Group Lite+ key, and LSE's documented answers where none was captured (`test_data/`, see its README). Nautilus' data testing spec (`docs/developer_guide/spec_data_testing.md`) has no case of custom data, and its `DataTester` requests none, so the custom data request is tested against the mock peer alone. With the `test-support` feature the peer and its payloads serve a caller's tests of its own fill through the adapter. They run with the workspace's, or alone:

```sh
cargo test -p lse
```

A test against LSE itself needs a key, so its caller holds and gates it, as this repository's caller does (`NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1`, see the root README).

## Data testing

`examples/node_data_tester.rs` runs Nautilus' `DataTester` on a live node with the client, against LSE itself with the keys of the environment or `.env` (`LSE_API_KEY`, `LSE_CME_API_KEY`), for the cases of Nautilus' [data testing spec](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader/blob/8b184c8af85068d788b591e10cb900fa2aba2cf2/docs/developer_guide/spec_data_testing.md) the adapter supports: TC-D01 (instruments), answered by the node's catalog, LSE publishing no definitions, into which the tester writes the crate's test definitions; TC-D21 (historical quotes), of GCZ6 of 2026, `GCZ26.GLBX`; TC-D31 (historical trades), of gold futures and of `GCZ26.GLBX`; and TC-D41 (historical bars), gold spot's 1-minute candles, over a past hour. The adapter streams nothing, so every subscription case is skipped, and every other request case, LSE having no call for it. The node runs until interrupted:

```sh
cargo run -p lse --features test-support --example lse-data-tester
```

Run on 13 November 2026 with the free key, over 10:00 to 11:00 on 12 November: TC-D01 answered both definitions from the catalog, the tester warning that their timestamps are 0, the test definitions being stamped at 1970; TC-D31 got 2,285 trades of gold futures, and none of gold spot, a commodity, which LSE stores no trades of (the client now fails that request with LSE's `data-unavailable`, "Tick trades are not stored for this market", rather than answer none; see [exports](#exports)); TC-D41 got 61 bars of gold spot, closing a minute apart from 10:00 to 11:00, at the nanosecond scale, with valid OHLC values. Run again on 13 November 2026 with the CME Group Lite+ key beside the free one, over the same hour: TC-D01 answered `GCZ26.GLBX`'s definition from the catalog too, with no timestamp warning; TC-D21 got 53,390 quotes of `GCZ26.GLBX`, bid below ask, with LSE's sizes, at the nanosecond scale, and the derived series' quotes failed with LSE's `400 data-unavailable` ("This market offers timeframes, including tick"); TC-D31 got 2,986 trades of `GCZ26.GLBX` with no aggressor side beside gold futures' 2,285; TC-D41 got its 61 bars as before.
