# LSE

A NautilusTrader adapter for London Strategic Edge's market data (LSE Data 3.0), laid out as Nautilus' adapters are: a data client only, as LSE trades nothing, naming no symbol. It serves the trades of LSE's derived markets and of US option contracts, their tick slices, as `TradeTick`s, LSE's candles of both, 1 second to 4 hours wide, as external `Bar`s stamped at their close, the trades and quotes of CME Globex contracts and of Nasdaq TotalView stocks, Level 1, as `TradeTick`s and `QuoteTick`s, their book, Level 2, as `OrderBookDepth` snapshots of ten levels a side, and their orders, Level 3, as `OrderBookDelta`s (see [book and orders](#book-and-orders)), and the rows of LSE's reference collections, of its macro series of releases and of yields and of an instrument's splits and dividends as custom data (`LseRow`, see [reference rows](#reference-rows)), and streams the quotes of its derived markets, each on the socket that carries it, as `QuoteTick`s (see [live quotes](#live-quotes)), the trades of US option contracts, through their underlying's chain on data-ws, as `TradeTick`s (see [live option trades](#live-option-trades)), CME Globex contracts on LSE's CME stream, Level 1 as `QuoteTick`s and `TradeTick`s, Level 2 as `OrderBookDepth` snapshots and Level 3 as `OrderBookDeltas` (see [live CME Globex](#live-cme-globex)), and Nasdaq TotalView stocks alike on LSE's Nasdaq stream, of the CME stream's protocol (see [live Nasdaq TotalView](#live-nasdaq-totalview)); every other request fails as Nautilus' data client default does (unsupported), and every other subscription is logged as not implemented. A caller checks the series it is to stream with `check_streamed` before anything connects, which refuses one the client does not stream, naming it and saying what LSE streams instead. A derived market's or a US option's past quotes are asked of LSE, which refuses them (`400 data-unavailable`: it serves their past as timeframes alone); an index's are the values of its tick slice (see [exports](#exports)). It decides nothing a key may have: it asks LSE, and LSE's refusal is the request's failure.

## Instruments

A series of a derived market (futures, stocks, indices, forex, commodities, crypto) is one instrument under the venue `LSEDGE`, not `LSE`, which is IB's London Stock Exchange. Its symbol is LSE's symbol, a hyphen and LSE's market in capitals: GOLD in `futures` is `GOLD-FUTURES.LSEDGE` (`LseSymbol`). LSE lists one symbol in several markets, and its market names have no hyphen, so the last hyphen splits symbol from market. An exchange market's listings (`cme`, `nasdaq`, `us_options`) are not `LSEDGE` series.

A CME Globex futures contract is the instrument Databento gives it, under `GLBX` (LSE's `XCME`), in Nautilus' two-digit year (`FuturesYearDigits::Two`): `ESZ26.GLBX` is the E-mini S&P 500 of December 2026. CME names a contract by its product, its month letter and its year's last digit, two for natural gas and, from 2030, crude oil, so a name comes round every ten years: LSE's `ESZ6` names the contracts of 2016 and of 2026. So a contract is asked of LSE by the number of its listing (`pair_id`), which names it alone, looked up once a client in LSE's catalogue (`/v3/pairs?market=cme&symbol=…`) under its year's last digit, then its last two (`ESZ6`, then `ESZ26`): the listing whose name says the contract's month (`ES futures 2026-12`) or, for one LSE names by its symbol alone (some delisted contracts, as `GCZ4` of 2024), the one delisted from three months before the contract's month to its end. A one-digit year, a root, a spread or an option is refused naming it. Nautilus' futures symbol functions (`identifiers::futures`) parse and write the names.

A Nasdaq TotalView stock is the instrument Databento gives it, under `XNAS`, the venue of Databento's `XNAS.ITCH`, Nasdaq's TotalView-ITCH feed, which Nautilus' IB adapter maps to IB's `NASDAQ`: its symbol is its ticker, so `NVDA.XNAS` is NVIDIA's common stock (`NasdaqStock`). Databento's raw symbols of `XNAS.ITCH` are in Nasdaq's convention, a share class after a dot (`BRK.B`, Databento's "CMS vs. Nasdaq symbology"), and LSE's `nasdaq` lists the same tickers (`/v3/pairs?market=nasdaq`, captured: `NVDA`, `BRK.B`), so a stock is asked of LSE by its ticker, with no lookup of its listing, and streamed under it. A ticker in lower case, which LSE would match but Databento never writes, or with a space, CMS's convention, is refused naming it. LSE answers a renamed ticker for the days it was listed (LSE's docs, "Symbols"), and its catalogue lists some tickers twice, a delisted listing beside a later one (`BRKH`, delisted on 18 December 2024, and listed now); its docs do not say which listing a reused ticker selects over dates of the earlier one, and Databento's id names the ticker alike, so such a stock's earlier history is LSE's answer for the ticker over those dates. LSE numbers a Nasdaq stock as it numbers the US stock (`pair_id`: NVDA is 2000008377 in both), which the client does not ask by.

A US option contract is the instrument Databento gives it, under `OPRA`, the venue of the `OPRA.PILLAR` publisher that sends its definitions, its symbol the contract's OSI symbol as Databento writes it: the root padded to six characters, the expiry's YYMMDD, C or P, and the strike in thousandths to eight digits, so `AAPL  261120C00355000.OPRA` is AAPL's 355 call of 20 November 2026 (`OpraContract`). LSE's US options (`us_options`) list it under that symbol, which names one contract, so it is asked of LSE by it, with the LSE key, which opens LSE's US options on the free plan. An id under `OPRA` that is no such symbol, unpadded as LSE's sockets write it, or its root in lower case, is refused naming it: Nautilus' OSI function (`osi_option_symbol`) writes the symbol again from its parts, and the two must match. Its definition is Databento's, whose OPRA record gives no multiplier, so Nautilus' Databento decoder makes it 1 (`decode_option_multiplier`), not the 100 shares of a standard contract. LSE stores the contracts' trades alone, as OPRA reports them, since October 2025.

A US option's greeks (`data=greeks`, one row a traded minute) are not served: their history waits for Nautilus to add a historical request of its `OptionGreeks`. At the pinned Nautilus, the catalog stores and replays `OptionGreeks`, but no request answers them: `DataResponse` has no variant of them, and the data engine's catalog write-back (`response_batches` in `crates/data/src/engine/streaming.rs`) writes none back, while `request_data`, the one request of other data, answers and writes back custom data alone, which `OptionGreeks` is not. So no request of a fill can carry them, and the adapter builds no other path for them, neither a catalog write of its own nor custom rows.

LSE's options chain and options flow (`/v3/options/chain`, `/v3/options/flow`) are not served either. The chain is the listed, unexpired contracts of an underlying as they stand, each with its last trade, today's volume and premium, and the greeks of its last traded minute; the call takes no dates, so it has no history, and a custom data request of it (`LseRow::data_type("options_chain", …)`) fails saying so before LSE is asked. Flow is a window's trades of an underlying's contracts, each with its premium (price × size × 100), its venue's letter, its days to expiry and the greeks of its minute; its trades are the tick slice's, which a trades request of the contract answers: 46 of `AAPL  261120C00355000` over 14:30 to 14:35 UTC on 12 November 2026, captured both ways with the free key on 13 November, matched one to one by time, price and size. The greeks of both, with the underlying's price they were computed at, are the rows of LSE's greeks export, field for field (the chain's 355 call's of 21:02 and flow's 325 put's of 14:30 that day, captured), so they wait with them for Nautilus. The rest of a flow row is its trade's (its premium the price times the size times 100, a standard contract's shares, not the multiplier of 1 Databento's definition gives), its symbol's (underlying, expiry, kind, strike, and days to expiry from the trade's day) or LSE's listing number (`pair_id`), but for its venue's letter, the OPRA participant that reported it, which no Nautilus trade carries and which is not served.

LSE publishes no instrument definitions. The client fails an instrument request naming the instrument, so a definition the catalog lacks fails where it is asked for (Nautilus' data engine asks the client only for one its catalogs lack), and converts LSE's prices and sizes at the precisions of the instrument in its cache, so the definition must reach the cache from elsewhere, such as a catalog Nautilus answers the instrument request from first or, for a CME contract or a Nasdaq stock, Databento's data client. A request of an instrument the cache lacks fails naming it, before LSE is asked.

## Credentials

LSE issues a key for its own markets and, with an exchange plan, a key of the exchange's, which authenticates only where its market is carried (`/v3/usage`, `product`). The client takes each, given in its config or read when the node creates the client: the LSE key, `api_key` or `LSE_API_KEY`, for the derived markets, a CME Group plan's key, `cme_api_key` or `LSE_CME_API_KEY`, for CME Globex, and a Nasdaq plan's key, `nasdaq_api_key` or `LSE_NASDAQ_API_KEY`, for Nasdaq TotalView (`LseProduct`). Any may be missing: a request of a market whose key is missing fails naming its variable, before LSE is asked, a client streaming a derived series with no LSE key, which the sockets take, a CME contract with no CME key, which LSE's CME stream takes, or a Nasdaq stock with no Nasdaq key, which LSE's Nasdaq stream takes, is not created, and nor is a client with no key. LSE refuses a key of another plan its market's rows (`403 plan-required`: the free key and the CME Group Lite+ key alike were told `Nasdaq Lite`, captured) and its stream (HTTP 401, `key-unknown`), and that refusal is the request's or the connection's failure. Each key goes in the `X-API-Key` header of its market's calls, and the CME and Nasdaq keys in that of their stream's opening request; no `Debug` output and no error holds it, and redirects are refused, so that it goes nowhere else.

## Exports

A trades request of a derived market's series or of a US option asks LSE's tick slice (`/v3/export`, `timeframe=tick`), the option's by its OSI symbol in `us_options` (LSE's `data=trades` belongs to its paid feeds); of a CME contract, its trades (`data=trades`), and a quotes request its quotes (`data=quotes`), each by its listing's number (`pair_id`); of a Nasdaq stock, its trades and its quotes alike, by its ticker in `nasdaq`, with the Nasdaq key. Each is asked as Parquet, at prices as published (`adjusted=0`), a UTC day an export over two timestamps, `[from, to)`. A request's window is closed, `[start, end]`, so it is asked as `[start, end + 1 ns)`, as Nautilus' Databento client asks one; a window ending at a midnight, as Nautilus' day windows from a midnight do, has that midnight's instant asked in its last day's export, LSE serving exports across midnight, rather than in an export of that instant alone. An export LSE refuses as too wide (`413 export-too-wide`: past the 2,500,000 rows one export may read on every plan, or not counted within LSE's 10 seconds) is asked again in halves, and a half LSE still refuses is halved again, down to a second, past which LSE's refusal fails the request: no second of any series LSE serves holds that many rows. LSE answers an export with its file: since 6 November 2026 it keeps no prepared file (`/v3/jobs` answers `export-unknown`), so there is no job to poll. A download cut short is fetched again, as LSE's docs say. LSE's file is taken only from a 200 carrying its SHA-256 (`x-checksum-sha256`), as LSE sends every file; any other success fails the request as an answer LSE's docs do not describe, with its status. A whole file whose SHA-256 is not the one LSE gives with it fails the request, naming the export by its selection and by LSE's id of it (`x-export-id`), for LSE to look into: LSE builds a file whole before sending it, so that its checksum is exact. One export downloads at a time, as LSE allows one a key.

Each key's calls are spaced to stay within the calls a minute of its plan in any minute, each key counting its own: the config's `calls_per_minute` for the LSE key, by default the free plan's 60, the fewest of any plan, `cme_calls_per_minute` for the CME key, by default CME Group Lite's 200, the fewest of its plans, and `nasdaq_calls_per_minute` for the Nasdaq key, by default Nasdaq Lite's 200, the fewest of its plans; set the plan's figure (`/v3/usage`, `calls_per_minute`) to go faster. Each key downloads one export at a time. A catalogue lookup is asked again for a refusal for the moment as an export is. A refusal for the moment, a 429 of calls or of the one download (`calls-exhausted`, `connections-exhausted`, `exports-busy`) or a busy 503, is asked again after the `Retry-After` LSE gives, or a backoff, ten times at most. A 429 of a quota (`allowance-exhausted`, `rows-exhausted`) fails the request at once with LSE's message: it lasts until Monday or until 00:00 UTC, and what the request did not get is the caller's to ask for again once it resets. A selection LSE holds no row of answers no rows: `date-not-trading`, and a `data-unavailable` whose message begins "No stored data for this selection", which LSE's docs give as the only one for a selection with no stored row; any other `data-unavailable`, such as a tick slice of a market storing no trades (forex, commodities) or a derived market's quotes, fails the request as other refusals do. An index has no trades: its tick slice holds its values with no size, so a trades request of an index fails naming it before LSE is asked, saying its ticks are quotes and its candles bars. A quotes request of an index asks its tick slice, each value a quote whose bid and ask are the value, with no sizes, as data-ws streams an index's ticks (see [live quotes](#live-quotes)), so that history and live give one type. Every other refusal fails the request with LSE's status, code, message and required plan, for example `403 plan-required: This feed requires a subscription; required plan: CME Group Lite`.

LSE stores a UTC day after it ends, the morning after it (its book and orders later still), and answers a day it has not loaded as one it holds no row of (`data-unavailable`: "the dates asked may not be loaded yet (or nothing traded in them)"). So the client takes the current UTC day as not loaded, whatever LSE answers, and the day before as not loaded until LSE answers rows of it. A window ending at a midnight reaches into the next day by that instant, and so into that span if the next day is in it. A request reaching into that span is answered with what LSE holds in a plain `DataResponse` rather than a completed `DataResponseEnvelope`, and the span is logged: the request completes, but Nautilus' data engine writes a payload-only reply to no catalog, as it certifies no coverage, so that a fill asks for the window again on its next run rather than recording a day LSE had not loaded as one with no rows. Nautilus' OKX client answers a window it cannot confirm the same way.

A bars request of a derived market's series or of a US option asks LSE's candles (`/v3/export`, `data=candles`, LSE's default) of the bar type's width, as published and as Parquet, as trades are; a CME contract's or a Nasdaq stock's bars are not asked, and fail naming them. LSE serves candles 1, 5, 15 and 30 seconds, 1, 3, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, and 1 and 4 hours wide, as `1-SECOND-LAST-EXTERNAL` to `4-HOUR-LAST-EXTERNAL` bars (`CandleWidth`), each of LSE's widths one bar specification: `60-MINUTE` is not `1-HOUR`. A candle has one price, the last: of trades where its market has trades, and of LSE's stored quotes in forex and commodities, which store no trades (LSE's docs; gold spot's candles have no volume). Any other bar type is refused naming it, before LSE is asked: a width LSE does not serve, another price, bars Nautilus builds (`INTERNAL`), and LSE's daily, weekly and monthly candles. LSE stamps those at their session's first traded minute and gives no close, and builds them from its minute bars, the same stored bars as its minute candles; their refusal says to declare the bar from a shorter LSE candle (1-MINUTE or 4-HOUR EXTERNAL) instead, which Nautilus aggregates on the calendar, each bar closed exactly. LSE stamps a candle at its open, on a grid of its width from 1970 in UTC; its bar is stamped at its close, its open plus its width (Nautilus' `BarSpecification::timedelta`), both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, as Nautilus' adapters stamp bars by default (Databento's `bars_timestamp_on_close`). A request over `[start, end]` gets the bars closing in it. LSE folds a candle from the rows inside the export alone, so a candle cut by the export's start comes partial: the client asks only whole candles, from the first one's open to the last one's close, a UTC day an export as trades are, and nothing if no candle closes in the window; an export of candles LSE refuses as too wide is halved between the two candles nearest its middle, and one of a single candle, which no width holds too many rows for, is not.

LSE sends prices and sizes as doubles. Each is converted once, through its shortest decimal form, which must fit the instrument's precision: a finer price fails the request rather than being rounded, as a definition too coarse would round it; a candle's volume is a size. A trade is stamped at LSE's time, both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, with no aggressor side, LSE sending none; its trade id is a hash (FNV-1a, as Nautilus' Databento adapter derives ids) of its instrument, time, price, size and its place among the trades alike before it in the file, so trades alike keep ids apart and a refill gives the same ids. A quote is stamped at LSE's time too, with the bid and ask sizes LSE sends; a quote with an empty side, a price LSE's schema allows to be empty, gives no `QuoteTick`, as Nautilus' Databento decoder gives none of a record with an undefined side (`decode_mbp1_msg`).

## Book and orders

A book depth request of a CME contract asks its book (`data=book`), Level 2, and a book deltas request its orders (`data=orders`), Level 3, each by its listing's number with the CME key, and of a Nasdaq stock, by its ticker with the Nasdaq key, as Parquet, a UTC hour an export rather than a day, as LSE's docs ask of a busy contract ("an hour or less of a busy contract at a time": ESZ6's book of the minute from 14:30 on 5 November 2026 held 99,104 rows, 33 MB as CSV, and of that whole day 10.8 GB); an hour LSE refuses as too wide is halved as any export is. LSE's estimate (`/v3/estimate`, asked on 13 November 2026, open to any key) of ESZ6's November so far gave its book 164.7 million rows in 4.94 GB of Parquet, about 30 bytes a row, and its orders 200.7 million in 3.21 GB, about 16, so a file at LSE's 2,500,000 rows an export weighs about 75 MB of book or 40 MB of orders, within the 100 MiB Nautilus' HTTP client takes of an answer (`HttpClient`, a limit its builder does not set at the pin: `max_decompressed_bytes` bounds a decoded body alone); a file past it fails the request with Nautilus' error rather than being halved. A plan that does not sell the level is refused by LSE, its refusal the request's failure with its required plan: CME Group Lite+, which holds Level 1 alone, gets `403 level-required: Level 2 book needs CME Group Standard or above; required plan: CME Group Standard`, and the same of Level 3 orders. A depth other than ten, the levels of LSE's book, fails naming it before LSE is asked, as Nautilus' Databento client serves MBP-10's alone. A book or orders request of an `LSEDGE` series is asked of LSE, which refuses it (`400 data-unavailable`).

LSE's book export is `time, sequence, action, side, price, size, depth`, then ten bid and ten ask levels of price, size and orders, best first, `bid_price_0`, `bid_size_0`, `bid_orders_0` to `ask_orders_9`, as LSE's Databank page reads a book's levels. Each row is the book after its event, and becomes an `OrderBookDepth` of its ten levels a side at the row's time and sequence, as Nautilus' Databento decoder makes one of an MBP-10 record (`decode_mbp10_msg`), keeping none of the event's own action, side, price, size or depth either; a level with no price is empty and no level of the snapshot. LSE's orders export is `time, action, side, order_id, price, size`: each order added (`A`), changed (`M`) or removed (`C`) becomes an `OrderBookDelta` of the order, by its id, side (`B` the bid, `A` the ask, `N` neither), price and size, as Nautilus' Databento decoder maps an MBO record (`decode_mbo_msg`), with no sequence, LSE giving none; a row of no action (`N`) gives none, as Nautilus' decoder gives none. Those are the codes LSE's CME stream documentation gives the same records ("The record"); any other fails the request naming it, a cleared book (`R`), which LSE gives at Level 2 alone, among them: LSE's docs do not say whether its orders open a day with a book, which a first capture will show, and the client invents none. LSE's exports carry no event boundary (the flags of its CME records, which its docs say mark the last of an exchange message), so the records of one time are one event, its last record flagged `F_LAST`, as Nautilus' Tardis loader frames the rows of one timestamp, and as Nautilus' order book delta contract asks of every event; a file never cuts a time. The columns whose type LSE's docs do not give, its counts, numbers, actions and sides, are read as the type Nautilus takes, whatever LSE types them as, a value that does not convert failing the request. Helix's keys hold no Level 2 or 3, and no Nasdaq plan, so these formats are LSE's documentation's, not a capture's (`test_data/`), and a Nasdaq stock's trades and quotes are taken in the columns of a CME contract's, as LSE's docs give one schema of each for both markets ("The selection"). LSE's docs give the codes of a CME record's actions; whether a Nasdaq stock's orders carry others, as Nasdaq's executions, is not documented, and an action other than `A`, `M`, `C` and `N` fails the request naming it, as for a CME contract.

## Reference rows

Every LSE row that is not a price is one custom data type, `LseRow`, made with Nautilus' custom data macros (`docs/concepts/custom_data.md`): its source, the collection, macro series or factors it is of (`bond_yields`), its symbol (`US10Y`), its time, and the row's other fields as LSE sent them, in JSON (numbers, strings, `null`, dates and times in ISO 8601). It is asked through Nautilus' custom data request, `request_data`, of a data type naming the source and the symbol in its metadata: `LseRow::data_type("bond_yields", "US10Y")`, which keys the series in a catalog by the identifier `US10Y-BOND_YIELDS`, LSE's symbol, a hyphen and its source in capitals, as an `LSEDGE` series' symbol names its market. The client answers with the request's data type, so the data engine writes the rows back under that identifier, and registers `LseRow` with Nautilus when it is created; a reader of stored rows registers it first (`LseRow::register`), as Nautilus decodes custom data by its type's name. A data type that is not `LseRow`'s, or names no source or symbol, or names LSE's options chain (`options_chain`), which has no history (see [instruments](#instruments)), fails before LSE is asked.

Its sources, by the `source` of the metadata:

- Each of LSE's 21 reference collections, by its name (`/v3/reference/export`), the `symbol` one of its series: the bond yields daily and at 1, 5, 15 and 30 minutes, an hour (`bond_yields_hourly`, and `bond_yields_intraday`, LSE's "Hourly bond yields") and 4 hours; sovereign yields, corporate bonds, credit indices and FX derivatives, a day's market values, a row a field (`symbol, bucket, date, field, value`: a bond's bid, ask and their day's range, yields, spreads, an option's implied volatility); insider trades; the earnings calendar; dividends and stock splits; company profiles and stock fundamentals, one snapshot of each company; the economic calendar (`US.Core Inflation Rate YoY`); the Commitments of Traders (`cot`, `ZN`); and financial reports. A source that is none of them, nor of the sources below, fails naming it before LSE is asked, and so does a collection LSE adds, until it is classified, rather than be stamped at its key's end, which a row published after its key would reach a reader early at.
- `macro_release` and `macro_yield`, LSE's macro series of releases and of yields (`/v3/macro/export`, `kind=release` or `yield`), the `symbol` its code (`US.Core Inflation Rate YoY`, `US10Y`). `macro_observation`, its series of observations, fails before LSE is asked, naming `macro_release`: an observation is keyed by the day of its period, not when it came out (US core inflation's September at 1 September, out on 14 October, and a weekly mortgage rate by the Thursday of its release), and LSE gives no release time of it, so it would reach a reader weeks early. Its releases hold the same values at the times they came out, from about 2015.
- `factors_splits` and `factors_dividends`, an instrument's splits and dividends as LSE's factors answer them (`/v3/factors`, whole, in JSON), the `symbol` LSE's symbol, a hyphen and its market in capitals (`AAPL-US_STOCKS`), as an `LSEDGE` series' symbol is written, its market asked in LSE's lower case; one with no market fails naming it before LSE is asked. A split's fields are LSE's (`price_factor`, `volume_factor`, `type`: `split` or `consolidation`), a dividend's too (`amount`, `declaration_date`, `record_date`, `payment_date`, `frequency`, `special`) with the `unit` of its amount, the factors' (`USD`, or `GBX`, pence); a futures series has no dividends. LSE gives the 500 newest dividends of an instrument alone (`truncated`), so a window from before the oldest it gave fails, naming the reference collection `dividends`, which holds them all; the collections `dividends` and `stock_splits` hold the same events by a bare symbol (`AAPL`, `GMS.L`).

A row is stamped when its values are all known, both `ts_event` and `ts_init`, so that no reader sees a value early (`Stamp`, a closed table of LSE's collections). Its key is the column LSE's export selects its rows by (a collection's `time_column` in LSE's catalogue, `/v3/reference`; a macro series' `day` or `released_at`; a factor's day):

- At its date's end, its 00:00 UTC plus a day, as a candle is stamped at its close (Nautilus' Databento decoder, `bars_timestamp_on_close`): the daily bond yields and the macro yields, a day's close, so that the 12th's reaches a reader at the 13th's 00:00; sovereign yields, corporate bonds, credit indices and FX derivatives, a day's market values; dividends and stock splits, of the collections and of the factors, keyed by the ex-date or the effective date, though announced before it; and the earnings calendar, keyed by the report date, LSE giving no report time. The earnings calendar's figures are as LSE holds them today, restated: AAPL's EPS of 1985 is 0.00161, split-adjusted by every split since, so ratios hold over time and absolute levels do not, and its `last_updated` is LSE's refresh, not when a figure came out.
- At 22:00 New York time of its date, the latest an SEC filing takes that day's date: insider trades, keyed by the day of the SEC filing that publishes them, a Form 3, 4 or 5 being filed on the same business day if submitted up to 22:00 Eastern time (Regulation S-T, Rule 13(a)(4)) and disseminated by EDGAR that evening, so that a trade filed on 13 October 2026 reaches a reader at 02:00 UTC on the 14th, or at 03:00 UTC while New York keeps winter time. The time zone is jiff's, from the tz database the crate bundles.
- At its interval's close, LSE stamping an interval at its open (`ts`): the bond yields at intervals, a 4-hour row of 20:00 at 00:00.
- At its key, the time it was known: company profiles and stock fundamentals, at `updated_at`, LSE's update of the snapshot; and the macro releases, at `released_at`.
- At a time of another column, its release: the economic calendar at `datetime`, in UTC, a release's time on the day of its key; a COT report of a Tuesday at the end of its `release_date`, the Friday it comes out, or, where LSE gives none (its reports since 28 April 2026), at `created_at`, LSE's ingest of it, about an hour after it came out; and a financial report, a statement (`income`, `balance`, `cashflow`) at its `accepted_date`, the SEC's acceptance of its filing, in UTC, and a row derived from the statements (`metrics`, `ratios`, `growth`) at the latest acceptance of the statements of its period (its date and `period`, `FY` or `Q3`). A row known at none of them, a release of no time, a report of no release date or ingest, or a derived report of a period of no statement accepted, such as the trailing twelve months' metrics and ratios (`TTM`, of their computation's date), is left out, with a warning naming it, rather than stamped at a time of the client's choosing.

A request over `[start, end]` asks the rows known in it and answers them in time order, the rows known outside it left out. A row known a fixed time after its key is asked by the dates of the keys of the rows known in the window, both included, as Parquet: LSE refuses a time (`filter-bad`) for a collection keyed by dates and selects a day's rows by its date either way. A row known at a time of another column may be keyed any time before it, so its series is asked whole up to the window's last date, so that none known in the window is missed: a COT report or a financial report published days or months after its key. A reference collection is asked after LSE's catalogue, for its time column. LSE stores a date the morning after it, as it does a market's day (see [exports](#exports)): if the last date of a key the window may hold is one it has not loaded yet, the current UTC day, or the day before while LSE holds no row of it, or, of a reference collection, a date after the last its catalogue says it holds (`last`, read with the collection's time column), which LSE loads days or weeks late for some (on 13 November 2026 it held insider trades filed up to 13 October, COT reports up to 6 October), the request is answered with what LSE holds in a plain `DataResponse`, which Nautilus' data engine writes to no catalog, and the span is logged, so that a fill asks for it again. A release LSE has not filled yet is a row of no `actual`: a window of the economic calendar or of a macro series of releases holding one, past, is answered so too, until LSE fills it (as of 13 November 2026, US core inflation's releases of 11 February and 10 November 2026 had no actual), so a window holding a release LSE never fills is asked again on every fill. An export is downloaded as a market's is: one at a time, checked by its SHA-256, a refusal for the moment asked again, and a selection LSE holds no row of (`data-unavailable`, "No stored data for this selection") answers no rows.

## Live quotes

The config names the instruments the client streams (`stream_ids`); none on a fill's node, which opens no socket. `connect` opens the socket each one's market streams on, one connection a socket, as LSE's limits ask, and each series streams under that socket's name of it, as LSE's socket documentation spells it ("Symbols on each socket") and as each socket acknowledged and ticked it on 13 November 2026: on data-ws, a US stock or a commodity under its symbol (`AAPL`, `BRK.B`, `XAU/USD`), a forex pair with a slash for its underscore (`EUR_USD` is `EUR/USD`), a futures series suffixed `.F` (`GOLD-FUTURES.LSEDGE` is `GOLD.F`) and an index `.IDX` (`SPX.IDX`); on london-ws, a UK stock suffixed `.L` and a crypto pair in dollars (`BTCUSDT` is `BTC/USD`); on tsx-ws, a Canadian stock suffixed `.TO`. A UK or Canadian stock's dots are hyphens there, a trailing one dropped (`BT.A` is `BT-A.L`, `BP.` is `BP.L`, `BBD.B` is `BBD-B.TO`). The fourth socket, chicago-ws, carries futures and forex, which data-ws carries whole, sending the same ticks, so no series streams there. data-ws lists four "US bond yields", `USB02Y/USD`, `USB05Y/USD`, `USB10Y/USD` and `USB30Y/USD`, with no name or category, but they tick the notes' prices, not yields (`USB10Y/USD` at 105.359 bid, 105.383 ask, captured on 13 November 2026 at 23:19 UTC, when the 10-year yield had closed at 5.133), and no market of LSE's HTTP API lists them, so no `LSEDGE` series names them and none is streamed: LSE's yields are rows of its reference collections and macro series, history alone. A series of a market no socket streams, or a crypto symbol not quoted in Tether, fails the client's creation, naming it. Each socket's address is LSE's own unless the config gives one (`base_url_ws_data`, `base_url_ws_london`, `base_url_ws_tsx`). It answers the socket's welcome with the key and is connected once LSE authenticates it, as Nautilus' adapter guide asks of `connect`. It fails, closing what it opened, with LSE's code and message if LSE refuses the key (`INVALID_KEY`) or a connection beyond the key's (`TOO_MANY_CONNECTIONS`, giving `max_connections`), naming the instruments the socket does not list to the key, LSE accepting a subscription to a symbol it does not list and sending nothing, and naming LSE's limit if the subscriptions of a socket's instruments, a symbol each series and a chain each underlying of the option contracts (see [live option trades](#live-option-trades)), are more than LSE says one connection of the key allows (`max_subscriptions`, 16 on the free key): LSE refuses one beyond it (`LIMIT_REACHED`) only once asked, after the node started.

A socket's list is its catalogue, which says what it may stream, not what it does: as captured on 13 November 2026, all four list one catalogue of stocks, ETFs, forex, commodities, crypto and indices beside their own entries, so data-ws lists crypto too, of which it sent only a tick of 8 November, crypto streaming on london-ws since; and tsx-ws lists one Canadian stock, `NVDA.TO`, of those it streams, sending `RY.TO`'s ticks unlisted. So a Canadian stock other than `NVDA.TO` fails the connect as unlisted.

A quotes subscription reads the instrument's precisions from the cache, failing naming it if the cache has no definition, then subscribes its symbol. Each tick becomes a quote of its bid and ask at the instrument's precision, each through its shortest decimal form, which must fit it, as an export's prices; a finer one is skipped with a warning rather than rounded. Its sizes are zero, LSE's socket ticks carrying none, and its price and volume are dropped: a futures tick's price is its bid. On Canadian stocks LSE's bid and ask both repeat the price, its feed carrying no quotes (LSE's socket documentation, "The tick"), and on indices they repeat the index's value, so their quotes have no spread. It is stamped at LSE's time of it (`ts_event`) and when it came (`ts_init`). LSE opens a subscription with the symbol's last tick, which comes through as any other. LSE's refusal of a subscription, such as `LIMIT_REACHED`, is logged as an error with its code and message, and the subscription is sent again on the next connection.

## Live option trades

A US option contract the config streams, an `OPRA` id, streams on data-ws in its underlying's chain: one subscription (`{"action": "subscribe_options", "underlying": "AAPL"}`) brings the ticks of every listed contract of the underlying, all strikes and expiries, and takes one of the connection's subscriptions however many contracts it has (LSE's socket documentation, "Option chains"; AAPL's held 3,233 on 13 November 2026). The underlying is the root of the contract's OSI symbol, and a contract's ticks name it by its OSI symbol without spaces (`AAPL261120C00355000`). A trades subscription of a contract reads its precisions from the cache, failing naming it if the cache has no definition, Databento's in a fill and so in a catalog, then subscribes its underlying's chain unless a contract of it is subscribed already; each tick of a contract subscribed becomes a trade, the chain's other ticks are dropped, and the chain is unsubscribed with its last contract's trades. LSE's US options feed carries trades alone ("The market: us_options"), and a tick's volume is the size of its trade (0 on a quote, which fails the tick): each becomes a `TradeTick` of its price and volume, at the contract's precisions, as an export's are, with no aggressor side, LSE sending none, stamped at LSE's time (`ts_event`) and when it came (`ts_init`), its trade id made as an export's trade's is, so that a trade LSE stamps alike live and in history gets one id. A chain sends no last prices: its first ticks are its contracts' next trades, so outside US options' hours none comes. A chain's subscription counts as one of the key's on its connection at `connect`; a socket lists no contract, so none is checked against its list, and LSE refuses an underlying with no listed options (`INVALID_UNDERLYING`, "No options available for …"), which is logged as an error, the chain sent again on the next connection, as a symbol's is. A trades subscription of a derived market's series fails naming it, its ticks being quotes (a futures tick's price is its bid). LSE's ticks of option contracts were not captured, US options being closed whenever the free key was at hand in this work: the tests replay ticks built from LSE's socket documentation's fields ("The tick") and the call's trades as LSE exported them, beside data-ws's acknowledgements of a chain and its refusal, which were captured (see `test_data/README.md`).

Each socket is a client on Nautilus' `WebSocketClient`, an outer client and the handler task owning it, as Nautilus' adapter guide lays one out ("WebSocket client patterns"). The application keepalive LSE asks for, `{"action": "ping"}`, goes every 25 s, as LSE's Python client sends it. A dropped connection is reconnected by Nautilus' transport with backoff (1 s, doubling to 30 s, with jitter); the client authenticates the key again, sends every subscription again (Nautilus' `SubscriptionState`), and logs the gap, from the dropped connection's last frame to the new one's authentication, leaving it as it is: nothing is fetched to fill it. A connection LSE closes for a refusal a new one cannot lift (`CONNECTION_REPLACED`, `KEY_IN_USE`, `KEY_REVOKED`, `QUOTA_EXCEEDED`) is not reconnected; one closed as beyond the key's connections is, with backoff, as LSE may still count the connection dropped.

## Live CME Globex

The config's `stream_ids` may name CME Globex contracts too, under `GLBX`: `connect` then looks each up in LSE's catalogue with the CME key, as a request does (see [instruments](#instruments)), its listing's symbol being the name LSE's CME stream takes it by, the HTTP API's (LSE's WebSocket documentation, "Contracts"), and connects to the stream, `wss://cme.londonstrategicedge.com/v1/stream` (`base_url_ws_cme` overrides it), the key in the opening request's `X-API-Key` header. A connection carries one subscription, its first message: the contracts at one level, in the `dbn` encoding, LSE taking one level a connection ("to change the contracts or the level, open another connection"). `connect` subscribes the contracts at Level 1, every trade and the best bid and offer, returns once LSE answers with `hello`, and fails with LSE's refusal: of a lookup, with its code and message, or of the subscription, which LSE closes with 1008 and its reason (`symbol unavailable: NGZ6`, a contract the stream does not carry); a key that is no CME Group key is refused with HTTP 401, which Nautilus' transport reports by its status alone. A contract whose listing LSE has delisted fails it too, naming the contract, before the stream connects, and so does a later subscription of one, logged as an error: the stream takes it by its listing's symbol, which names the later contract of that name now (`ESZ16.GLBX` is `ESZ6`, which carries 2026's), so it would stream another contract's records under its id; its history is asked by its listing's number, which names it alone. Level 2, a contract's ten best levels a side after each change of one, and Level 3, its order events, each take a connection of their own, which the first book depth or book deltas subscription opens, as Nautilus' Databento client opens a schema at its first subscription; the subscriptions made while it connects join its first message. LSE's refusal of it, `CME Levels 2 and 3 need CME Standard or above` for a key of a plan without them, CME Group Lite+'s among them, closes it with 1008: the client logs it as an error, naming the level and the contracts, and a later subscription of that level asks LSE again on a new connection.

LSE opens each contract with its last best bid and offer at Level 1, its last ten levels at Level 2 and nothing at Level 3 ("the stream carries order events and no opening book"), then streams its records, each a binary frame: LSE's 24-byte header (`L3DB`, its version, a snapshot or live kind, the contract's sequence, the stream's number for the contract and a count of records), then the record, Databento's `Mbp1Msg`, `Mbp10Msg` or `MboMsg`, as the connection's level is, which the client reads with the `dbn` crate. Each record becomes Nautilus data through Nautilus' Databento decoder. At Level 1 (`decode_mbp1_msg`): its best bid and offer a `QuoteTick`, with the sizes LSE sends, and its trade, if it is one (action `T`), a `TradeTick` with no aggressor side, LSE sending side `N` for every trade ("the feed does not say which side took it"), at the price precision of the contract's definition in the cache, a finer price failing the record rather than rounding. The decoder stamps both at the record's `ts_recv`, when LSE received it, as Nautilus' Databento client stamps its data, and `ts_init` when the record came; LSE's exports stamp a CME trade or quote at its exchange time, earlier (GCZ6's trades of 21 October 2026: 0.17 ms to 347 ms, median 0.29 ms, before Databento's, which are stamped at `ts_recv` too), so a contract's live data and its history from LSE differ in that. At Level 2 (`decode_mbp10_msg`): its ten levels a side after the record's change, an `OrderBookDepth` with each level's count of orders, an empty level (no price) none, as a book export's row is (see [book and orders](#book-and-orders)). At Level 3 (`decode_mbo_msg`): the order an event adds (`A`), changes (`M`) or removes (`C`), an `OrderBookDelta` of its id, side, price and size, as an orders export's row is; an event of no action (`N`) changes no order and gives none. A contract's deltas start at its first order event and none is invented, its book unknown before it. LSE flags the last event of each exchange message (128, `F_LAST`), so the client gathers a contract's deltas until it and sends them as one `OrderBookDeltas`, as Nautilus' Databento client sends a live event's; a flagged event of no delta ends its message too, its `F_LAST` put on the message's last delta, as Nautilus' Databento decoder does on history, so that each event ends `F_LAST` (the adapter guide's delta contract). The decoder stamps depths and deltas at `ts_recv` too, where a book or orders export stamps a row at its exchange time. It numbers a trade, a depth and a delta by the record's own sequence, which LSE leaves 0; the client gives each the stream's sequence of its record instead, one of its contract's and level's alone (a trade's id, a depth's and a delta's `sequence`).

A quotes, trades, book depth or book deltas subscription of a contract reads its price precision from the cache, failing naming it if the cache has no definition, and takes that data of the contract's records from then on; a contract's records before any subscription are not sent. A quotes or depth subscription opens with the contract's latest best bid and offer or ten levels, LSE's opening or a later record's, unless they went out already. A depth other than ten fails naming it, as Nautilus' Databento client serves MBP-10's ten levels alone; the book type of a deltas subscription is not read, LSE's orders being Level 3, as Databento's client serves MBO for any. A contract the stream does not carry at a level, a later contract of a rolled root, is looked up and carried from a new connection of that level, since LSE takes one subscription a connection: the client asks Nautilus' transport to reconnect, and the new connection's subscription names it beside the others, which open again. An unsubscription stops that data of the contract; the stream carries it on, LSE taking no unsubscription on a connection, until the level's connection is replaced, whose subscription leaves out a contract taking no data of the level: an expired contract named there, a rolled root's old contract, would have LSE refuse the whole subscription (`symbol unavailable`, closing the connection with 1008, which is not reconnected) and stop the level's data.

LSE gives each contract's records a sequence, one more than the last. A jump is a gap, as when the stream drops the records a slow reader could not take: it is logged, naming the records that did not come, and left, nothing standing in for them. A dropped connection is reconnected by Nautilus' transport with backoff (1 s, doubling to 30 s, with jitter); the new one sends the subscription again, each contract opens again, and an opening beyond the contract's last record is passed on, the records between logged as a gap; one that brings nothing new is passed over. A new `epoch` in LSE's `hello` is a reset of its feed, whose sequences start again: the reset is logged and its gap left. The client pings every 15 s, as LSE pings it, so that a dead connection is replaced. A close for a refusal a new connection would not lift, 1008 (the subscription refused) or 4001 (the key replaced or revoked, or its plan ended), is not reconnected, and logged as an error, the level's next subscription opening a new connection; every other is, among them 4003 (the account's connections all held), 1012 (LSE's feed connection reset) and 1013 (a contract not ready yet). A gap at Level 3 can leave a book built from the deltas wrong, an order whose removal did not come staying in it, LSE sending no book to mend it.

Each connection is a client on Nautilus' `WebSocketClient`, an outer client and the handler task owning it, as for the sockets, Nautilus' `SubscriptionState` holding the contracts carried; the handler connects the transport itself, once it carries a contract, so no subscription overtakes the connection.

## Live Nasdaq TotalView

The config's `stream_ids` may name Nasdaq TotalView stocks too, under `XNAS`: `connect` then connects to LSE's Nasdaq stream, `wss://nasdaq.londonstrategicedge.com/v1/stream` (`base_url_ws_nasdaq` overrides it), with the Nasdaq key, which "sends the same messages for stocks" as the CME stream (LSE's WebSocket documentation, "The CME Group stream"), so the stream's client, its records and their decoding are the CME stream's (see [live CME Globex](#live-cme-globex)), a connection of its own for each level and its own state, beside the CME stream's: a stock's Level 1 best bid and offer and trades, Level 2 ten levels a side and Level 3 order events become `QuoteTick`s and `TradeTick`s, `OrderBookDepth`s and `OrderBookDeltas` alike, through Nautilus' Databento decoders, at the precision of the stock's definition, Databento's of `XNAS.ITCH`. A stock is subscribed under its ticker, LSE's catalogue asked nothing, a ticker naming its stock (see [instruments](#instruments)); one the config does not stream is carried from the level's next connection when first subscribed, as a later CME contract is. LSE's documentation gives a trade's side as `N`, "the feed does not say which side took it", of the CME stream; a Nasdaq record's side, if LSE sends one, becomes the trade's aggressor side as Nautilus' decoder reads it (`B` a buyer, `A` a seller). Helix holds no Nasdaq plan, so nothing of the Nasdaq stream but its refusal was captured: the tests replay NVDA's sessions built from LSE's documentation of the CME stream's messages (`test_data/`).

## Tests

The tests run against mock LSE peers (`testing`), an HTTP server, a socket, one for each of LSE's sockets a test streams on, and LSE's CME stream, replaying payloads and frames captured from LSE with the free key and the CME Group Lite+ key, and LSE's documented answers where none was captured (`test_data/`, see its README): LSE's documented ESZ6 session stands for a CME contract's live records, captured after CME's Friday close, when none came, each record written as LSE's `dbn` encoding sends it, which the test support's conversion reproduces byte for byte on GCZ6's opening, captured in both encodings; GCZ6's Level 2 and Level 3 sessions, built from LSE's documentation of their messages, stand for the levels helix's key does not hold, LSE's refusal of both to that key captured; and NVDA's exports and sessions at Levels 1 to 3, built from LSE's documentation, stand for Nasdaq TotalView, which no key of helix's holds, LSE's refusals of its rows and its stream to helix's keys captured. Nautilus' data testing spec (`docs/developer_guide/spec_data_testing.md`) has no case of custom data, and its `DataTester` requests none, so the custom data request is tested against the mock peer alone. The streamed quotes and option trades are tested as the spec asks, its `DataTester` driving the client on a node against the mock socket: TC-D20 (quotes after start), TC-D30 (an option contract's trades after start, from its underlying's chain) and TC-D70 (their subscriptions ended on stop, the chain's with its contract's); and a CME contract's and a Nasdaq stock's, against the mock CME and Nasdaq streams: TC-D20, TC-D30 (trades after start, with no aggressor side) and TC-D70, and TC-D12 (book depth, ten levels a side, opening with the last ten) and TC-D10 (book deltas, from the first order event, each batch ending `F_LAST`, at `L3_MBO` into the tester's managed book), each with TC-D70; TC-D21, a CME contract's historical quotes, runs against LSE in the [data tester](#data-testing). `DataTester` requests no history of book deltas or depth, so a custom actor asks the client for a CME contract's and a Nasdaq stock's on a node against the mock peer, as the spec asks historical book deltas to be tested until it does; helix's keys hold neither level, nor any of Nasdaq TotalView, so there is no run of them, history or live, against LSE. With the `test-support` feature the peers and their payloads serve a caller's tests of its own fill and live node through the adapter. They run with the workspace's, or alone:

```sh
cargo test -p lse
```

A test against LSE itself needs a key, so its caller holds and gates it, as this repository's caller does (`NAUTILUS_LSE_RUN=1`, see the root README).
